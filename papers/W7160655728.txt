Abstract 17 
Background 18 
Non-canonical open reading frames (ncORFs) encode a substantial, largely unexplored layer of 19 
 2 
the human proteome. The short microproteins they produce frequently escape detection by 20 
standard mass spectrometry (MS) workflows, creating a “dark proteome” that hampers gene 21 
annotation and functional characterisation. To address this, sequence-derived attributes were used 22 
to identify factors associated with MS repository detectability and to build an interpretable 23 
predictive framework that enables rational prioritisation of ncORFs for proteomic discovery. 24 
Results 25 
7,097 ncORF sequences (99 % <150 residues) were curated from the TransCODE Consortium, 26 
and labelled by PeptideAtlas peptide-level evidence as MS-detected (n = 1,725) or undetected 27 
(n = 5,372). An ensemble combining ESM-2 protein language model embeddings with logistic 28 
regression and nineteen biological features (ESM2-LR) achieved a mean ROC-AUC of 0.721 29 
(SD 0.018, coefficient of variation 2.5 % across ten independent runs). Model-agnostic SHAP and 30 
permutation importance analyses concordantly ranked cumulative in-frame ribosome footprint 31 
uniformity (CIF-HBP) as the strongest associated factor, followed by mammalian ORF-level 32 
evolutionary conservation (ORBLv_mammal) and genomic biotype. Predicted HLA class I-binding 33 
peptide density, which captures physicochemical properties overlapping with optimal LC-MS/MS 34 
analyte characteristics, was also positively associated with detectability. Late-fusion analysis 35 
demonstrated that ESM-2 embeddings and biological features contribute complementary predictive 36 
signals. 37 
Conclusions 38 
These findings indicate that sustained translation, purifying selection for ORF structural integrity, 39 
and favourable peptide physicochemistry converge to influence ncORF detectability. The ESM2-LR 40 
framework provides a reproducible, scalable tool to prioritise candidate microproteins for targeted 41 
MS validation, gene annotation, and the interpretation of disease-associated regulatory variants, 42 
subject to future external validation on independent datasets. 43

1. Introduction 45 
 46 
Proteins encoded by well-annotated, protein-coding genes, collectively termed the canoni cal 47 
proteome, account for only a subset of the sequences actively translated in human cells. Recent 48 
evidence indicates that numerous additional sequences, not annotated as canonical, undergo active 49 
translation, revealing a largely unexplored layer of the proteome. However, the detect ion and 50 
annotation of these non-canonical open reading frames (ncORFs) remain challenging, underscoring 51 
the need to expand gene catalogues and improve our understanding of their roles i n human 52 
physiology and disease. Over the past decade, evidence from ribosome profiling (Ribo- seq), mass 53 
spectrometry (MS)-based proteomics, and immunopeptidomics has revealed tha t thousands of 54 
ncORFs are actively translated in human cells, producing short polypeptides termed micropro teins 55 
or peptideins (candidate microproteins for which a physiological role cannot yet be verified) [1]), or 56 
small ORF-encoded peptides (SEPs) [1-7]. Ribo-seq evidence shows translation, but ribosome 57 
occupancy does not always result in stable protein accumulation. This study specifically examines 58 
detection in mass spectrometry repositories as an operational proxy for productive t ranslation that 59 
yields stable, detectable protein products. 60 
Non-canonical ORFs constitute a heterogeneous class of genomic elements defined by their 61 
derivation from previously not annotated genomic regions as primary protein-coding sequences. 62 
They include upstream ORFs (uORFs) located in the 5′ untranslated regions of messe nger RNAs, 63 
where they are translated by scanning ribosomes before reaching the main coding sequence; 64 
upstream overlapping ORFs (uoORFs) that share partial sequence with an annot ated coding 65 
sequence in an alternative reading frame; internal ORFs (intORFs) embedded wi thin canonical 66 
 4 
coding sequences; downstream and downstream overlapped ORFs (dORFs and doORFs); ORFs 67 
encoded within long non-coding RNAs (lncRNA-ORFs); and novel intergenic or repetitive element-68 
derived ORFs [8-12]. Each biotype class has a mechanistically distinct relationship with the ribosome 69 
and a correspondingly different probability of yielding a stable, detectable translatio n product [13-70 
17]. 71 
Despite their widespread translation, few ncORFs have been formally annotated as canonical 72 
protein-coding genes by reference annotation authorities such as GENCODE and UniProtKB/Swiss-73 
Prot. This annotation gap reflects two converging challenges: first, the low an d often condition-74 
dependent expression of ncORF-encoded microproteins, which places man y below the detection 75 
threshold of standard MS-based proteomics workflows; and second, the frequent absence of amino 76 
acid sequence conservation across species, which has historically been a prerequisite for confident 77 
protein-coding gene annotation [1]. Furthermore, because reference annotation authori ties 78 
increasingly require independent mass spectrometric evidence to elevate transcript-level O RFs to 79 
protein-coding gene status, understanding the factors associated with MS detectabi lity has direct 80 
consequences for gene annotation pipelines and prioritisation criteria. However, it is now recognised 81 
that conventional measures of protein-coding constraint may be insufficient for the ncORF space, 82 
where evolutionary selection may act primarily to maintain the structural features of the ORF, such 83 
as the presence of an open reading frame, a functional initiation codon, and a proper termi nation 84 
codon, rather than conserving the specific amino acid sequence encoded [1, 2, 18]. 85 
Functionally characterised microproteins have been shown to regulate muscle physiology, cardiac 86 
function, mitochondrial metabolism, and cell cycle progression [19-20]. In the oncology context, 87 
ncORF-derived peptides have been identified as cancer-restricted cryptic antigens presented by 88 
Human Leucocyte Antigen (HLA) class I molecules, which categorises them as potential 89 
immunotherapy targets [21-24]. Furthermore, disruption of uORF translation by genetic variants has 90 
 5 
been linked to human Mendelian diseases and cancer predisposition, underscoring the regulatory 91 
importance of this translational layer [25-27]. 92 
Despite their biological significance, ncORF-derived microproteins represent a chal lenge for MS-93 
based proteomics analysis. Their short length limits the number of tryptic pe ptides available for 94 
detection, and often lacks "proteotypic" peptides, those that are uniquely identifiable and possess 95 
high ionisation efficiency, further exacerbating the detection gap. The absence of these 96 
microproteins from standard proteome databases leads to their frequent exclusi on from database 97 
search strategies [4, 28-30]. The result is a substantial ’dark proteome’ of translated products not yet 98 
detected by conventional proteomics workflows, largely due to low expression and database 99 
exclusion. 100 
The launch of international consortia, including the TransCODE Consortium, the HUPO Human 101 
Proteome Project (HUPO-HPP), the Human ImmunoPeptidome Project (H UPO-HIPP), and the 102 
GENCODE annotation group, reflects the growing recognition that a systemati c, evidence-based 103 
framework is required to characterise the ncORF-encoded proteome. Such a framework is needed 104 
to determine which ncORF products merit annotation as bona fide protein-coding genes or, at 105 
minimum, as peptideins, candidate microproteins or translation products that are con fidently 106 
detected endogenously but for which a physiological role cannot yet be verified [1, 18, 31]. 107 
Understanding how sequence, physicochemical properties, translation and evolutionary signatures, 108 
and genomic context influence the repository MS detectability of ncORF products is therefore 109 
essential. Such knowledge enables the rational prioritisation of ncORFs for annotation , the 110 
optimisation of targeted MS or immunopeptidomics strategies to investigate the dark proteome, and 111 
the discrimination of likely functional microproteins from the large pool of lowly expressed or non-112 
functional ncORF translations [1, 5, 32-35]. 113 
 6 
In this study, we developed a computational framework based on Evolutionary Scale M odelling 2 114 
(ESM-2). Protein language models (pLMs) like ESM-2 can capture latent biophysical constraints 115 
(e.g., fold stability, surface accessibility) that are difficult to encode manually, particularly for peptides 116 
in the 8–25 residue range typical of ncORF tryptic products. These embeddings may complement 117 
hand-crafted physicochemical features for predicting ncORF microprotein detection, though their 118 
marginal contribution remains to be quantified. ESM2-LR systematically evaluated sequence-derived 119 
features, including translation evidence, genomic biotype, evolutionary conservation, seconda ry 120 
structure, physicochemical and biophysical properties, and immunogenicity, across 7,097 ncOR F 121 
sequences. The aim was to identify the strongest sequence and annotation-derived factors 122 
statistically associated with cumulative MS repository detection. 123

2. Background and Rationale 125 
2.1 The non-canonical proteome and its detection challenge 126 
Non-canonical ORFs originate from genomic regions not annotated as primary pro tein-coding 127 
sequences and can be classified into several distinct biotype classes, each with a unique relationship 128 
to the ribosome: 129 
• ORF biotype1: upstream overlapping ORF (uoORF), located in the 5’ UTR of mRN As and 130 
overlapping the primary coding sequence in an alternative reading frame; associated with 131 
translational regulation. 132 
• ORF biotype2: upstream ORF (uORF), located in the 5’ UTR of mRNAs and translated by 133 
scanning ribosomes before the main coding sequence; also associated with translational 134 
regulation. 135 
 7 
• ORF biotype3: internal ORF (intORF), embedded within canonical mRNAs. 136 
• ORF biotype4: lncRNA-ORF, a short ORF within long non-coding RNA transcripts, typically 137 
translated at low efficiency but occasionally producing functional microproteins. 138 
• ORF biotype5: downstream ORF (dORF), located downstream of the main coding sequence. 139 
• ORF biotype6: processed transcript (PT). 140 
• ORF biotype7: downstream overlapping ORF (doORF), overlapping the primary coding 141 
sequence. 142 
Each biotype class varies in its likelihood of producing a stable, abundant peptide detectable by MS, 143 
and this heterogeneity poses a central challenge for predictive models th at aim to operate across 144 
the entire ncORF space. Here, ’detectability’ refers specifically to peptide-level evidence in 145 
aggregated MS repositories (PeptideAtlas), integrating biological abundance, cellular stab ility, and 146 
technical detection parameters. 147 
Correspondence between biotype taxonomy and the numerical biotype indicator columns used in 148 
the 7,097 ncORF analytical dataset. Biotype indicators orf_biotype1, orf_biotype4, and orf_biotype5 149 
are the only three biotype columns present in the complete-case feature set derived from GENCODE 150 
v42 annotations following the definitions in [1]. Note that the sequential numbering (biotype1–7) in 151 
this section refers to the full GENCODE biotype taxonomy; the orf_biotype1/4/5 column labels in the 152 
dataset correspond to specific subsets of this taxonomy. 153 
Sequences may be positive for more than one biotype indicator. Sequences negative fo r all three 154 
indicators belong to other biotype classes (biotype2 uORF, biotype3 intORF, biotype6 PT, biotype7 155 
doORF) that were not included as separate features in this analysis; their absence as features does 156 
not preclude their presence in the dataset. 157 
 8 
 158 
2.2 Mass spectrometry detectability of short peptides 159 
In standard bottom-up LC-MS/MS proteomics, peptide detectability depends on several factors: 160 
sufficient protein abundance to surpass the instrument’s noise threshold, generation o f tryptic 161 
peptides within the optimal 7 –25 amino acid range for electrospray ionisation and fragmentation, 162 
adequate hydrophobicity for reverse-phase chromatography retention, and suitable charge state 163 
distribution for precursor selection. Importantly, most of the 7,097 analysed ncORF-deri ved 164 
sequences are shorter than 150 amino acids and typically yield only 1-3 tryptic peptides, which 165 
substantially reduces their likelihood of detection in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) workflows that 166 
prioritise abundant peptides for fragmentation. 167 
 168 
2.3 Feature selection rationale 169 
Given these constraints, MS detection of ncORF-derived peptides is influenced not only by classical 170 
physicochemical properties, which may be inadequate for short sequences, but also by translation 171 
evidence, evolutionary conservation, translational mechanisms, and other factors aff ecting 172 
detectability. Accordingly, features such as ncORF Translation Uniformity (CIF-HBP), ORBLv (ORF 173 
Relative Branch Length - evolutionary conservation), biotype, and predicted HLA-binding affinity 174 
were included in the analysis to comprehensively capture these influences. 175

3.1 Dataset construction 179 
A total of 7,097 ncORF-derived amino acid sequences, 99% of which comprise few er than 150 180 
residues, were curated from the TransCODE Consortium reference dataset [1]. Sequence s were 181 
classified as MS-detected (positive class; n = 1,725) or MS-undetected (n egative class; n = 5,372) 182 
based on peptide-level evidence consolidated from two publicly accessible repositories: the Human 183 
non-HLA PeptideAtlas build 2023-06 (https://peptideatlas.org/builds/human/non-hla/) and the 184 
Human HLA PeptideAtlas build 2023-11 (https://peptideatlas.org/builds/human/hla/index_ 2023-185 
11.php), as described in [1]. The resulting class imbalance (~1:3.1 positive-to-negative) reflects the 186 
biologically realistic low detection rate of ncORF-derived microproteins in cumulative MS repositories 187 
and represents the natural prevalence against which the model’s practical utility should be assessed. 188 
Performance metrics evaluated on the balanced test sets (PR-AUC, F1-score) do not directly 189 
generalise to this natural prevalence. 190 
Biotype annotations (biotype classes 1, 4, and 5 as defined in Section 2.1) were derived from 191 
GENCODE v42; the original TransCODE dataset used GENCODE v35 [1]. CIF_HBP (cumulative in-192 
frame footprint uniformity score, Human Body-map P-site; see Section 3.2) was obtained for all 7,097 193 
ncORFs from ribosome profiling data of the Human Body Map multi-tissue dataset, as previously 194 
defined [2]. ORBLv evolutionary conservation scores were computed for all 7,097 ncORFs as 195 
described in [1]. NetMHCpan 4.1 HLA class I binding affinity predictions (IC ₅₀, nM) were pre-196 
computed across a representative panel of common HLA alleles; n_predHLAbinders_div_aaLength 197 
represents the number of predicted binders with IC₅₀ < 500 nM per amino acid length of the ncORF, 198 
as previously described [1]. A systematic limitation of the MS-detection labels must be 199 
acknowledged: PeptideAtlas and ProteomeXchange aggregate detections across heterogeneous 200 
experimental conditions, tissues, cell lines, and instruments. An ncORF classified as ’undetected’ 201 
may be genuinely expressed in untested contexts, constituting label noise that biases t he classifier 202 
boundary and contributes to the ROC-AUC performance ceiling (see Discussion, Section 5.4). 203

3.2 Feature engineering 208 
In addition to the ncORFs amino acid sequence, 19 features were computed per sequence, spanning 209 
four categories. 210 
i. Physicochemical and sequence-compositional features. 211 
Amino acid length; average residue volume (AvrgVolResidues); electron ion interaction potential 212 
(ElectrIonInteracPotentialVal); free energy of solvation in water (FreeESol_inWater) ; instability 213 
index; log₁₀-transformed relative mutability (log10RelMutab); netCharge; Normalised frequency 214 
of alpha helix (NormFreqAlphaHelix); Normalised frequency of beta sheet, un weighted 215 
(NormFreqBetaSheetUnweight); Ratio of average and computed composition 216 
(ratioAvrgComputedComp); SolubilityIndex. These attributes were computed using the 217 
Bio.SeqUtils.ProtParam module from BioPython and Amino Acid Indices version 9.2 218 
(https://www.genome.jp/aaindex/). 219 
ii. Translation signature. 220 
CIF_HBP, the cumulative in-frame footprint score from the Human Body -map P-site dataset, 221 
quantifies the proportion of codons in an ORF at which in-frame ribosomal P-site occupancy 222 
exceeds 33.3% of the maximum P-site density observed across that ORF. It captur es whether 223 
ribosomes traverse the entire ORF throughout their length (uniform elongation) rather than 224 
initiating at the 5′ end and stalling or terminating prematurely. CIF_HB P is derived from 225 
population-averaged Ribo-seq data across the Human Body Map dataset and does not reflect 226 
 11 
condition-matched translation; its association with MS repository detectability is best interpreted 227 
as a constitutive translation signature rather than a condition-specific efficiency estimate. 228 
iii. Evolutionary features. 229 
(a) ORBLv_mammal: ORF Relative Branch Length - evolutionary conservation of ORF structural 230 
integrity across mammalian genomes, specifically, the preservation of a functional initiation 231 
codon, an uninterrupted reading frame, and a stop codon, without requiring con servation of the 232 
encoded amino acid sequence. (b) PhyloCSF: conventional amino acid-level coding potential 233 
scored by comparative phylogenetic substitution modelling (codon substitution model). (c) 234 
Upstream Kozak context conservation. (d) mean_constraint: mean per-nucleotide evolutio nary 235 
constraint derived from the Evo 2 genomic foundation model [36]. For each ncORF, the genomic 236 
context (minimal–maximal exon boundaries – 1,000 nucleotide flanking sequence) was retrieved 237 
from GRCh38 via the Ensembl REST API. Evo 2 computed, at each nucleotide position, the log-238 
likelihood of the reference base relative to each alternative base; a per-position constraint score 239 
was defined as the minimum log-likelihood difference across the three alternative substitutions. 240 
The mean of this score across all positions within the ncORF genomic context constitutes 241 
mean_constraint. The more negative this score is, the more constrained. Ful l derivation details 242 
are provided in the Supplementary Information (Supplementary Figure 5). 243 
iv. Genomic and translational features 244 
Binary biotype class indicators (orf_biotype1 [uoORF], orf_biotype4 [lncRNA-ORF], orf_biotype5 245 
[dORF]); number of predicted tryptic peptides; fraction of tryptic peptides falling within the 8–25 246 
amino acid window optimal for LC-MS/MS detection; maximum predicted HLA-I bi nding affinity 247 
among all tryptic peptides (IC ₅₀; NetMHCpan 4.1); n_predHLAbinders_div_aaLength (see 248 
Section 3.1). 249

For a complete enumeration of the 19 biological features used in the ESM2 -LR classifier, and their 250 
importance ranking, see Supplementary Information (Supplementary Figure 5). 251

3.3 Feature Correlation Analysis 253 
Pairwise Spearman correlations were computed across all 19 numeric features in the complete-case 254 
dataset (n = 7,097 ncORFs) to identify collinear associated factors that could cause misattribution of 255 
shared variance in the downstream importance analyses. The collinearity flagging threshold was set 256 
at |r| > 0.70. No feature pair exceeded this threshold (maximum observed: |r| = 0.648 between 257 
PhyloCSF and aa_length). No very strong correlations (|r| > 0.90) were observed across any of the 258 
171 pairwise comparisons. Full results are presented in Figure 2 (Spearman correlation heatmap). 259 
The absence of collinear feature pairs confirms that all 19 importance scores derived from SHAP 260 
LinearExplainer are directly attributable to their respective features without shared-v ariance 261 
correction. The two domain-specific features central to this analysis, CIF_HBP (max |r| = 0.256) and 262 
mean_constraint (max |r| = 0.215), were among the most independent in the entire set, confirming 263 
that they contribute genuinely orthogonal biological information. 264

3.5.2 Sequence embedding with ESM-2 305 
 306 
i. Model selection and loading 307 
Protein language model embeddings were generated using ESM-2 308 
(facebook/esm2_t6_8M_UR50D): 6 transformer encoder layers; 8 million pa rameters; 320-309 
dimensional hidden states; pre-trained on the UniRef50 database at 50% sequence identity 310 
clustering [37]. This variant was selected to balance representational capacity with com putational 311 
efficiency for the target sequence length distribution (all sequences ≤ 128 amino aci ds). The 312 
tokeniser and pre-trained model weights were loaded from the Hugging Face Mode l Hub via 313 
AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained and AutoModel.from_pretrained, respectively. The mod el was 314 
placed in evaluation mode (model.eval()) for all inference operations, disabling dropou t and 315 
suppressing gradient computation. The ESM-2 8M variant may have insufficient rep resentational 316 
capacity compared with larger variants (35M–3B parameters); a comparative ablation across ESM-317 
2 sizes is recommended in future work. 318 
ii. Embedding generation procedure 319 
 16 
Sequences were tokenised with a maximum length of 128 amino acid residues (padding = 320 
’max_length’; truncation enabled). A single forward pass extracted hidden st ates from all six 321 
transformer layers (indices 1–6). Mask-pooled mean aggregation (weighted by the attention mask, 322 
excluding padding tokens) yielded one 320-dimensional embedding vector per layer per sequence. 323 
Per-layer embedding matrices of shape [N × 320] were cached as NumPy (.np y) arrays for each 324 
dataset split (training, validation, test) to avoid redundant computation. 325 
Layer subset selection employed a zero-fit Fisher Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) criter ion. For 326 
each of the 6 layers, class- conditional mean vectors (μ ₀ˡ, μ₁ˡ ∈ ℝ³²⁰), the between-class difference 327 
vector (δˡ = μ ₁ˡ − μ ₀ˡ), and the ridge -regularised pooled within- class covariance matrix (Σˡ; 328 
regularisation ε = 10 ⁻⁶) were computed on the training set. Cholesky decomposition pre -computed 329 
Σˡ⁻¹δˡ per layer. All 63 non -empty subsets (2⁶ − 1) were scored analytically under a block -diagonal 330 
independence approximation: J(S) = Σ_{l∈S} δˡᵀ(Σˡ)⁻„δˡ. The top 6 subsets by J(S) were validated by 331 
logistic regression AUC on the validation set. Layers 2–6 were selected as the optimal combination. 332 
iii. Ensemble combined stream 333 
Multiple balanced training sets were generated via random undersampling of the majority class, each 334 
with a distinct random seed (10 seeds total). For each seed, selected layer embeddings (layers 2–335 
6; 5 × 320 = 1,600 dimensions) were concatenated with the 19 biological features to form the 336 
combined feature matrix [n × 1,619]. A logistic regression classifier with L2 penalty was trained with 337 
hyperparameter optimisation: GridSearchCV over C ∈ {0.001, 0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 338 
0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 100} (14 values), 5-fold stratified CV, libline ar solver, ROC-AUC scoring. 339 
Validation and test set predicted probabilities were recorded per seed; final combine d-stream 340 
predictions were averaged across ensemble members. 341 
A biological-feature-only baseline ensemble was constructed in parallel, using the same 10 -seed 342 
undersampling procedure, with logistic regression trained on the 19 biological fea tures scaled by a 343 
 17 
robust scaler (to mitigate outlier influence). Late fusion combined the two streams via a convex 344 
combination: p_fused = α · p_combined + (1 − α) · p_bio. The mixing coefficient α was optimised on 345 
the validation set by grid search over [0.0, 1.0] in steps of 0.05, maximising validation ROC-AUC. A 346 
classification threshold was subsequently tuned on the validation set by scanning [0.30, 0. 70] in 347 
steps of 0.005, maximising- score. The optimised α and threshold were applied unchanged to the 348 
held-out test set. 349 
Methodological note: Biological features were unscaled in the combined stream but RobustScaled 350 
in the bio-only baseline. Because L2-regularised logistic regression is scale-sensiti ve, this 351 
inconsistency confounds the interpretation of the fusion weight α and may distort relative feature 352 
importance estimates. 353

v. Feature importance analysis 367

v. 2 Permutation importance analysis 422 
Each of the 19 biological features was independently permuted (values randomly shuffled across 423 
test-set samples) with 20 repeats per feature; the resulting mean decrease in test -set ROC-AUC 424 
was recorded per feature (n_jobs = −1). Features causing larger AUC decreases are considered 425 
more important. Mean and SD across repeats were visualised as horizontal bar charts with error 426 
bars (Figure 1B). Permutation importance is treated as a cross-validation too l to confirm top-tier 427 
SHAP rankings; it is not the primary importance estimate. Negative permu tation importance values 428 
(observed for FreeESol_inWater) are interpreted as artefacts of test-set statistical power limitations 429 
or collinearity, not as evidence of genuine detrimental contributions, which SHAP correctly resolves 430 
to a small positive attribution. 431

4.3 Top Predictive Features 523 
The top predictive features of MS repository detectability were identified by two com plementary 524 
model interpretation methods: SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlanations) via LinearExplainer , and 525 
permutation importance (Figure 1). The five highest-ranked features by SHA P analysis were: (1) 526 
Translation Uniformity (CIF_HBP); (2) genomic biotype indicator for lncRNA- ORF membership 527 
 32 
(orf_biotype4); (3) mammalian ORF-level evolutionary conservation score (ORBLv_m ammal); (4) 528 
amino acid sequence conservation score (PhyloCSF); and (5) downstream ORF membership 529 
(orf_biotype5). Upstream overlapping ORF membership (orf_biotype1) ranked sixth in both methods. 530 
Predicted HLA-binding peptide density (n_predHLAbinders_div_aaLength) ran ked ninth by SHAP 531 
and eighth by permutation importance. Concordance across attribution methods increases 532 
confidence that these features carry a reproducible statistical signal, though this does not establish 533 
causal relationships with MS detection. 534 
For a linear classifier operating on a combined ESM-2 embedding and numerical featur e matrix, 535 
SHAP via LinearExplainer is the primary importance method. It computes exact, analytically derived 536 
attributions from the model’s coefficient vector and the estimated feat ure covariance structure, 537 
producing values that reflect each feature’s contribution within the actual l earned input manifold. 538 
Permutation importance serves as an independent validation tool. The concordance of top rankings 539 
between the two methods constitutes the most reliable finding of the importance analysis. 540 
Notably, the number of predicted tryptic peptides and the fraction within the optimal 8–25 aa window 541 
were not among the top-ranked features, likely because most ncORFs in this dataset have similarly 542 
limited tryptic peptide yields (1–3 peptides), minimising inter-sequence variance. 543

ncORFs, which typically yield only 1 –3 tryptic peptides, uniform occupancy may increase the 564

4.6 Comparison of SHAP and Permutation Importance and 766 
Methodological Justification 767 
SHAP and permutation importance analyses yielded largely concordant rankings, with three features 768 
occupying identical ranks in both methods: CIF_HBP (rank 1), orf_biotype4 ( rank 2), and 769 
ORBLv_mammal (rank 3). This three-way convergence across independent attribution methods 770 
provides the strongest available evidence that these features carry a genuine association signal for 771 
ncORF MS repository detectability and are not artefacts of any single interpretability ap proach. 772 
orf_biotype1 also ranked identically (rank 6) in both methods. While ranks 1 –3 are robust, mid-tier 773 
rankings (ranks 4 –9) show method-dependent variation and should be interp reted with greater 774 
caution. 775 
Mid-tier discordances, particularly for PhyloCSF (SHAP rank 4, permutation rank 5) and 776 
n_predHLAbinders_div_aaLength (SHAP rank 9, permutation rank 8), are mechanist ically 777 
interpretable. For the linear classifier evaluated here, SHAP via LinearExplainer is the primary 778 
reliability standard for three reasons: (i) SHAP values are analytically exact for linear models, 779 
computed directly from the coefficient vector and feature covariance structure without sampl ing 780 
noise; (ii) the correlation-dependent perturbation mode correctly accounts for the correlated structure 781 
of the 320-dimensional ESM-2 embedding space, whereas permutation importance tr eats all input 782 
dimensions as exchangeable and thereby distorts the attributed importance of correlated embedding 783 
clusters; and (iii) the negative permutation importance observed for FreeESol _inWater is a 784 
diagnostic signal of insufficient test-set statistical power or correlation artefact rather than a genuine 785 
negative contribution, which SHAP correctly resolves to a small but positive attribution. Permutation 786 
importance is therefore treated as a cross-validation tool for the top tier of results, and its agreement 787 
at ranks 1–3 and rank 6 provides high-confidence confirmation of those features. Rankings below 788 
rank 6 should be interpreted with greater caution and are not the basis for bi ological conclusions. 789 
While biological features were screened for pairwise collinearity, ESM-2 embedding dim ensions 790 
 50 
encode compositional information that may correlate with biological features (e.g ., hydrophobicity). 791 
The ’interventional’ SHAP mode assumes feature independence, which may marginally misattribute 792 
shared variance between embeddings and biological features. 793

5. Discussion 814 
The central finding of this study is that, within a sequence- and annotation-de rived feature space, 815 
cumulative MS-based repository detection of ncORF-derived microproteins is statistically associated 816 
with three features: the uniformity of active translation across the ORF length, evolutionary selection 817 
at the ORF structural level, and the physicochemical properties of tryptic pepti des relevant to both 818 
HLA class I presentation and LC-MS/MS ionisation. The integration of ESM-2 protein language 819 
model embeddings with logistic regression (ESM2-LR) achieved F1 = 0.706 after validati on-set 820 
threshold optimisation. The best traditional classifier (MLP) achieved ROC-AUC = 0.720 but was not 821 
threshold-tuned for F1, precluding fair comparison. Any apparent ESM2-LR advantag e rests 822 
primarily on the F1-score, which is confounded by threshold asymmetry; the ROC-AUC difference is 823 
too small to be interpreted as a meaningful performance advantage. Technical factors, incl uding 824 
instrument sensitivity, sample preparation, and database completeness, were not modelled and 825 
represent an additional source of unresolved variance. 826 
The complementary nature of ESM-2 embeddings and biological features is evident from the late-827 
fusion architecture: the validation-optimised fusion weight α averaged 0.525 across stability runs (SD 828 
= 0.155), indicating that neither information stream alone captures all the relevant signal. The F1 829 
advantage of ESM2-LR over Linear SVM (0.706 vs. 0.488) may suggest that ESM -2 embeddings 830 
contribute sequence-level information relevant to true-positive identification that bi ological features 831 
cannot provide, improving sensitivity without a proportional increase in false positives. ESM 2-LR 832 
may be suitable for large-scale screening applications where follow-up validation r esources are 833

5.2 Evolutionary Features: ORF-Level Conservation Dissociates from 865 
Amino Acid Conservation 866 
ORBLv_mammal, which captures preservation of ORF structural integrity (start codon, uninterrupted 867 
reading frame, stop codon) across mammalian genomes without requiring amino acid sequence 868 
conservation, ranked third by both SHAP and permutation importance, and showed a prog ressive 869 
enrichment of detected ncORFs at higher conservation values. This pattern is consistent w ith the 870 
TransCODE Consortium finding [1] that ORF-level constraint may indicate pur ifying selection to 871 
maintain translational competence, though it does not exclude RNA-level or regulatory functions as 872 
alternative selective pressures. If ncORFs under ORF-level purifying selection sustain uniform 873 
translational elongation, then this hypothesis could be examined by combining ORBLv and CIF_HBP 874 
distributions. Alternatively, high ORBLv may correlate with more efficient transcriptional or 875 
translational machinery (e.g., stronger Kozak contexts), leading to higher protei n abundance and 876 
thus greater MS detection probability, rather than purifying selection on the encoded peptide per se. 877 
PhyloCSF, which scores amino acid-level coding potential by comparative phylogenetic substitution 878 
modelling, ranked fourth by SHAP with a negative directional effect, but showed no coherent 879 
monotonic trend in detection fraction across its value range; both detected and undetected ncORFs 880 
were overwhelmingly distributed at negative PhyloCSF values. The negative SHAP con tribution of 881 
PhyloCSF most plausibly reflects the logistic regression coefficient applied to a featu re that is 882 
 54 
predominantly negative-valued and collinear with amino acid length (|r| = 0.648): sequences with 883 
slightly less negative PhyloCSF values are not more detectable; rather, the feature acts as a length-884 
correlated covariate within the learned decision boundary. This dissociation between 885 
ORBLv_mammal (positive, rank 3) and PhyloCSF (negative effect, rank 4) suggests that detectable 886 
ncORFs are more likely to show evidence of selection for ORF structural integrity than for amino acid 887 
sequence conservation as measured by conventional phylogenetic metrics [16]. An alternative 888 
hypothesis is that many ncORFs are simply young, lineage-specific ORFs that have not yet 889 
accumulated amino acid substitutions, and ORBLv captures phylogenetic signal noise rather than 890 
purifying selection. Distinguishing these hypotheses requires population genomic analysis: genuine 891 
purifying selection should manifest as reduced polymorphism and depletion of loss-of-f unction 892 
variants in high-ORBLv ncORFs, whereas phylogenetic noise would show no such population-level 893 
constraint should be observed. This alternative could be tested by correlating ORBLv with Kozak 894 
sequence strength or translation initiation rates independent of ORF structure. 895
