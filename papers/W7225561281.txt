ABSTRACT
Traditional scheduling assumes a single actor, while real-world scenarios often involve multiple 
decision-making units with conflicting objectives, requiring collaboration, coordination, negotia-
tion, and collective intelligence. Multi-agent scheduling (MAS) provides a realistic framework for 
modelling such interactions under shared resources. This article presents a comprehensive sciento-
metric and systematic review of two-agent and multi-agent scheduling, examining the field’s devel-
opment paths, knowledge structure, and emerging research clusters. Main path, cluster, and key-
word analyses are integrated to trace knowledge diffusion, identify influential studies and research 
branches, and examine how collaborative intelligence is manifested across scheduling contexts. The 
findings reveal an evolution from early complexity-oriented single-machine and dual-agent mod-
els toward richer production environments, advanced computational methods, and application-
oriented MAS in smart manufacturing, energy systems, edge computing, and decentralised project 
scheduling. More importantly, the synthesis reveals a broader transition from optimizing com-
peting agent-specific objectives toward interaction-driven collaborative scheduling intelligence 
based on coordination, negotiation, information sharing, adaptation, and collective learning. This 
domain-level perspective suggests that future MAS should integrate decentralised decision-making, 
adaptive intelligence, and system-level coordination to reconcile local objectives with system-wide 
performance.

1. Introduction
Multi-Agent Scheduling (MAS) provides decision sup-
port for short-term planning and control in systems 
with multiple actors that compete for the use of the 
same resources to achieve conflicting goals (Allesan-
dro Agnetis et al. 2004). Such settings frequently arise 
in real-world industrial systems, where decentralised 
decision-making, resource contention, and conflicting 
stakeholder objectives create operational challenges that 
traditional single-agent scheduling approaches cannot 
effectively address. Prime examples of competing agents 
(i.e. decision-makers) in real-world scenarios include 
transportation and logistics, for instance, when the sup-
ply chain of critical products is under pressure and sup-
pliers, manufacturers, and retailers compete for logistics 
resources (Ying, Pourhejazy, and Sung 2024); healthcare, 
where different hospital departments compete for med-
ical resources (Zuo, Jin, and Liu 2024); and portfolio 
management, where projects or investments compete for 
limited human, material, or financial resources (Gholami

cloud computing platforms, heterogeneous agents com-

These practical challenges highlight the role of MAS 
as a modelling and optimization framework capable of 
capturing interaction-driven behaviours and supporting 
coordinated decision-making in complex systems.
When agents have different objectives, game-theoretic 
behaviour may influence the operational dynamics, and 
hence, accounting for it becomes necessary to model 
the inherent interactions. In this situation, including 
either synergistic or conflicting individual objective func-
tions, and ensuring the feasibility of the schedules for 
every agent, are the major scheduling requirements 
(Lang, Fink, and Brandt 2016). These features exacer-
bate mathematical complexity and complicate the solu-
tion spaces of scheduling problems. Preliminary inves-
tigations revealed that more than 600 articles have con-
tributed to the development of modelling and solution 
algorithms in MAS. A review of these studies may facili-
tate the evolution of MAS to tackle modern problems.
The existing reviews are specific to a particular type 
and/or application of MAS, such as resource alloca-
tion and scheduling in smart grids (Binyamin and Ben 
Slama 2022), or methods for decomposing large-scale 
railway scheduling problems (Leutwiler and Corman 
2023). In the broadest review (Perez-Gonzalez and Fram-
inan 2014), the authors systematically surveyed multi-
objective MAS problems, with particular attention to 
their mathematical complexity and solution algorithms, 
thereby providing a structured basis for understanding 
methodological developments in MAS research. T o the 
best of our knowledge, no prior review has systemat-
ically employed scientometric methods – particularly 
Main Path Analysis (MPA) and Cluster Analysis (CA) 
– to examine the evolutionary development paths of 
MAS. Moreover, the most relevant review article was 
published more than 12 years ago; therefore, an updated 
and methodologically rigorous review of MAS is both 
timely and necessary.
This study makes two primary contributions to MAS. 
The main contribution is to draw the big picture of 
MAS developments based on the MPA of the litera-
ture, considering the main development paths and key 
branches (John S. Liu, Lu, and Ho 2019). The signifi-
cant mathematical features and solution algorithms are 
reviewed to shed light on the main patterns of devel-
opment and the collaborative intelligence manifested 
in the ways multiple agents coordinate, negotiate, and 
reconcile competing objectives under shared resource 
constraints. In this review, ‘collaborative intelligence’ is 
defined as a system-level, interaction-driven scheduling 
capability that emerges when multiple autonomous or 
semi-autonomous agents coordinate their actions, nego-
tiate decisions, share information, adapt dynamically to 
changing environments, form collaborative coalitions, or 
learn collectively to reconcile decentralised local objec-
tives under shared resources and constraints. Impor-
tantly, collaborative intelligence is not universally pre-
sumed to be inherent in all MAS studies. MAS formula-
tions that merely model multiple independent job sets or 
mathematically competing objectives without incorpo-
rating decentralised coordination, adaptive interaction, 
information exchange, negotiation, or learning mecha-
nisms are not regarded as representations of collaborative 
intelligence in the present review. Accordingly, collab-
orative intelligence is treated as a structured analyti-
cal lens for systematically interpreting interaction-driven 
scheduling behaviours rather than as a universal prop-
erty of all multi-agent scheduling models. Operationally, 
collaborative intelligence is identified through observ-
able coordination mechanisms, the information struc-
ture among agents, the adaptation or learning processes 
involved, and the resulting system-level scheduling out-
comes, such as feasibility, fairness, responsiveness, and 
overall performance improvements. From this perspec-
tive, many early two-agent scheduling studies primarily 
belong to the category of competitive multi-objective 
scheduling, in which agents coexist mathematically but 
do not necessarily exhibit explicit collaborative, adap-
tive, or decentralised interaction mechanisms. Neverthe-
less, these studies remain structurally important because 
they systematically established the theoretical and com-
putational foundations upon which later coordination-
oriented and learning-enabled MAS systems were devel-
oped.
Second, the major subfields in MAS are discussed to 
explore the status and speculate on emerging trends and 
research directions. CA (Girvan and Newman 2002) is 
used as a basis for suggesting future research directions 
in an objective way. By integrating trajectory analysis 
(i.e. MPA), structural analysis (i.e. CA), and temporal 
analysis (overlay and timeline visualization), this study 
offers a comprehensive and multi-dimensional under-
standing of the evolution of MAS, thereby capturing its 
structural, developmental, and temporal dynamics from 
complementary perspectives.

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Search and review protocols
The dataset was constructed from the W eb of Science 
(W oS) database using a systematic keyword search strat-
egy combined with predefined inclusion and exclusion 
criteria to ensure relevance and quality. The resulting 
publications were used to construct a citation network, 
which serves as the foundation for analyzing the main 
development paths. For MPA, traversal weights were 
computed using the Search Path Link Count (SPLC) 
metric to identify the most significant knowledge dif-
fusion paths, including both the Global Main Path and 
Key-route Main Path. Specifically, MPA was conducted 
using MainPath494S, in which the SPLC algorithm was 
adopted as the traversal-weighting scheme. The Global 
Main Path was extracted using the Global Search and 
Standard modes, whereas the Key-route Main Path 
analysis was subsequently performed to complement 
the Global Main Path by capturing multiple signifi-
cant knowledge-diffusion trajectories that might not be 
represented by a single global path. Neither the key-
route threshold nor the number of significant routes was 
manually specified; therefore, the default software con-
figuration implemented in MainPath494S was retained. 
CA was performed using the Group Finder function 
in MainPath494S with the Edge-Betweenness Clustering 
algorithm to partition the citation network into struc-
turally related groups of articles. T o ensure methodolog-
ical consistency and analytical reproducibility, no user-
defined clustering parameters were introduced, and the 
default software configuration was adopted throughout 
the clustering process. The combined use of MPA and 
CA enables the knowledge domain to be interpreted from 
both structural-evolutionary and thematic perspectives, 
thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding 
of the development path of collaborative intelligence in 
multi-agent scheduling.
The following search query was used to collect 
the review dataset from the W oS databases through 
T opic Search: TS = (‘multi-agent∗’ OR ‘multi agent∗’ 
OR ‘multiagent∗’ OR ‘two-agent∗’ OR ‘two agent∗’) 
AND TS = (‘scheduling∗’). A total of 1,841 articles 
published between 2004 and early 2025 were initially 
retrieved. T o improve the reproducibility and method-
ological rigour of the review protocol, 36 review articles 
were first excluded to avoid citation biases that could 
distort the identification of the main development path. 
Subsequently, isolated records lacking sufficient citation 
connectivity, along with irrelevant publications, were 
excluded during the screening process.
After the screening process, 628 articles remained for 
the subsequent MPA and CA. This stepwise procedure 
is also summarised in Figure 1 to enhance the repro-
ducibility, transparency, and methodological clarity of 
the review protocol. The precision index (Precision =

2.2. Identification of the main development paths
Citations in academic research can be attributed to 
knowledge transfer. In a citation network, tail/head
nodes refer to the cited/citing articles. In the network 
view, source nodes are knowledge initiators, intermediate 
nodes propagate knowledge, and sink nodes represent the 
most recent advances in the field of study. The number of 
times a specific knowledge transfer (citation) is traversed 
shows its importance. This considers all possible paths 
from all ancestors of the tail node, and itself, to all paths 
leading to the sink nodes (John S. Liu, Lu, and Ho 2019). 
MPA identifies key papers based on citation relation-
ships, specifically by measuring their traversal frequency 
along knowledge diffusion paths (e.g. Search Path Link 
Count, SPLC), which is adopted in this study because it 
captures all possible source-to-sink traversal paths rather 
than relying on raw citation counts, thereby providing a 
more comprehensive representation of knowledge diffu-
sion than alternative metrics such as Search Path Count 
(SPC). In the present study, the MPA was conducted 
using MainPath494S, where the SPLC algorithm was 
employed as the traversal-weighting scheme because it 
effectively identifies the principal knowledge-diffusion 
trajectory within a citation network. The Global Main 
Path was extracted using the Global Search and Standard 
modes. Furthermore, to enhance the robustness of the 
analysis, this study combines global MPA with Key-route 
MPA, where the former captures the most structurally

dominant trajectory, whereas the latter identifies influ-
ential but structurally peripheral branches that might 
otherwise be overlooked due to inherent constraints in 
the citation network. This combined design mitigates 
the inherent structural bias of citation networks and 
enhances the coverage of both dominant and emerging 
knowledge trajectories.
Inspired by the idea of analyzing literature through 
network science (Hummon and Dereian 1989), path 
analysis of MAS helps identify the trajectories that con-
stitute the most prominent knowledge transfers in the 
development of the field. Through this kind of analy-
sis, one can systematically trace theoretical evolution, 
methodological development, and changes in research 
trends. Nevertheless, like other citation-network meth-
ods, MPA remains affected by the temporal lag in citation 
accumulation. Newly published studies may be under-
represented because they have not yet generated suffi-
cient citation links to be fully embedded in the network, 
even when they are methodologically strong or techni-
cally significant. T o mitigate this recency-related limi-
tation, the present study complements the Global Main 
Path with Key-route MPA and CA, thereby broadening 
the coverage of emerging and less dominant knowledge 
trajectories.
In this study, the Global Main Path and the Local 
Main Paths across different application areas are analyzed 
separately. The Global Main Path method enumerates 
all possible citation paths and selects the path with the 
largest traversal weight, thereby identifying the struc-
turally dominant knowledge-diffusion trajectory instead 
of simply highlighting the most highly cited papers 
(Mrvar and Batagelj 2016). The Global Main Path of 
MAS consists of 19 representative documents, includ-
ing 3 source nodes (green), 14 intermediate nodes (red), 
and 2 sink nodes (blue), as shown in Figure 2. Connect-
ing arrows represent citation relationships between doc-
uments. Consistent with the commonly used labelling 
convention for identifying article nodes in main-path 
and citation-network visualizations, each article in Fig-
ures 2–3 is represented by a compact identifier consisting 
of the first author’ s surname, the initials of the remaining 
authors’ surnames, and the publication year. For exam-
ple, ChengNY2006 denotes the article by Cheng, Ng, and 
Y uan published in 2006. These labels are used solely as 
article-node identifiers in the MPA and as cross-reference 
keys linking the network nodes in Figures 2–3 to their 
corresponding discussions in the text; they do not consti-
tute an alternative bibliographic citation style. The con-
ventional author–year citation is retained when the arti-
cle is discussed in the text, thereby allowing readers to 
trace a study from its node in the main-path network to 
the corresponding textual discussion and, ultimately, to 
its complete entry in the reference list.
The main path articles are considered structurally 
influential publications within the knowledge-diffusion 
network and are systematically reviewed and analyzed in 
Section 3. Importantly, the studies reviewed in Section 3 
are selected based on their structural roles in transmitting 
knowledge along the developmental path, rather than 
solely on their raw citations. The Key-route Main Path 
method is further employed to analyze the branches that 
emerge from the Global Main Path. Instead of starting 
only from source nodes, this method expands the analysis 
from the most highly traversed links, thereby identifying 
influential branches that may not appear on the Global 
Main Path (John S. Liu and Lu 2012). The Key-route 
Main Path was generated using the default configuration 
of MainPath494S, without manually specifying the key-
route value, traversal threshold, or number of significant 
routes, thereby ensuring methodological consistency and 
analytical reproducibility. The resulting citation network 
was subsequently exported in Pajek-readable format for 
visualization and complementary network analysis. As 
shown in Figure 3, the key-route path consists of 49 docu-
ments. This complementary analysis is particularly useful 
for capturing technically important studies – including 
recent and emerging works – that have not yet attained 
structural prominence within the Global Main Path. 
Accordingly, the articles outside the Global Main Path are 
treated as secondary yet still influential research branches 
and are therefore systematically reviewed in Section 4.
2.3. Identification of the main research clusters

tion relationships among the articles in the review 
dataset. Clustering is performed using the GroupFinder

introduced, and the default software configuration was 
retained throughout the clustering process. The resulting 
article clusters were subsequently interpreted to iden-
tify the major thematic structures and emerging research 
directions within the knowledge domain. Importantly, 
keyword analysis was not used to construct these clus-
ters. Instead, after the citation-network clusters had been 
identified, keyword co-occurrence analysis was subse-
quently performed using VOSviewer to characterise and

2.4. Logistic growth model
The cumulative number of publications was modelled 
using a logistic growth model (McAllister 1998) to char-
acterise the overall short- to medium-term evolution-
ary trend of research on multi-agent scheduling. The 
logistic growth model is expressed as N(t) = K/(1 +
e−r(t−t0)), where N(t) represents the cumulative num-
ber of publications in year t, K denotes the estimated 
saturation level, r represents the intrinsic growth rate, 
and t0 is the inflection point corresponding to the max-
imum publication growth rate. The model parameters 
were estimated using a nonlinear least-squares estima-
tion procedure based on the cumulative publication data 
from 2004 to 2024. The estimated model parameters were 
K =42,219,013.62, r =0.157706, and t0 =2092.97, with 
R2 =0.9685 and RMSE =40.15. The growth curve analy-
sis and indicative forecast of MAS publications are shown 
in Figure 4.
As shown in Figure 4, research on MAS has not 
yet reached maturity, indicating substantial potential for 
future research. Because the fitted inflection point lies far 
beyond the observed data period, the forecasting curve

4. Review of the key development branches
Following the main development trends, several key 
branches highlight influential ideas and niche applica-
tions that complement the Global Main Path. These sec-
ondary trajectories are important because they capture 
technically significant developments that are structurally 
connected to the field but may not become dominant 
on the Global Main Path, particularly when they are 
relatively recent. The early branches mainly focused on 
deepening modelling constraints and expanding multi-
objective interactions. Later branches emphasised algo-
rithmic diversification and the introduction of new solu-
tion strategies. The most recent branches illustrate the 
transition from theoretical modelling to practical inte-
gration and intelligent decision support, particularly in 
reinforcement learning-based scheduling, decentralised 
negotiation, and edge–cloud-enabled applications.

processing (1|2A, compatible batching|f1, f2 ≤ Q). Both 
agents minimised their maximum cost, total comple-
tion time, and (weighted) delayed job count. The sec-
ond agent’ s objective value was considered a constraint. 
A fully polynomial time approximation algorithm was 
developed for the case when both agents are aiming for 
a weighted delayed job count.
W angFZZL2017 (Jun Qiang W ang et al. 2017) studied 
a parallel-batching machine with two agents, where jobs 
have the same processing time but different sizes. The 
objective was to minimise the makespan of the first agent 
and limit the second (PBM|2A, equal processing time,
variable job size|Cmax1, Cmax2 ≤ Q). It was proven that 
there is no polynomial approximation algorithm with a 
finite worst-case ratio to solve this problem unless P =
NP. They developed a lower bound algorithm and two 
heuristics for optimization.
YinYWCW2018 (Yin et al. 2018) incorporated inven-
tory management variables into integrated scheduling 
of production and batch delivery operations in a sin-
gle machine environment, where the first agent’ s opera-
tions are determined by the decision-maker, and the due-
date of the second agent is exogenous (1|2A, batching,
inventory, due date assignment |f1, f2 ≤ Q). Computa-
tional complexity was analyzed for various scenarios, and 
both exact and approximate solutions were developed to 
solve the problem for different objective functions.
4.3. Advanced modeling and algorithmic 
developments
Earlier branches emphasised model theory and basic 
assumptions; the more recent studies extend the theoret-
ical foundations of two-agent and multi-agent schedul-
ing by incorporating increasingly complex schedul-
ing features, heterogeneous objectives, and resource-
sharing constraints, together with the development of 
exact, approximation, and pseudo-polynomial solution 
methods.
LiuDLWKCW2019 (Liu et al. 2018) explored a two-
agent non-preemptive single machine scheduling prob-
lem with release time conditions, aiming to minimise 
the linear combination of the makespans of the agents 
(1|2A, rj|α · Cmax1 + β · Cmax2). A new approximation 
algorithm was first developed to solve the problem. A 
fully polynomial-time approximation scheme is con-
tributed to improve the solution quality.
Y angL2019 (Y ang and Lu 2019) studied a two-agent 
scheduling problem with position-dependent process-
ing time, accounting for the learning and deteriorating 
effects, to minimise the makespan of one agent when 
the maximum makespan of the other is constrained 
(1|2A, PDPT|Cmax1, Cmax2 ≤ Q). They showed that the 
problem is NP-hard and designed a polynomial-time 
algorithm to handle scenarios with learning or deteri-
orating characteristics. Besides, dynamic programming 
and fully polynomial-time approximation methods were 
developed to minimise the total makespan of a single

agent. They extended the model for parallel-machine 
environments and solved it using dynamic programming.
Y uanNC2020 (Y uan, Ng, and Cheng 2020) ana-

and preemption features, when each agent’ s objec-
tive is to minimise its own max-form regular objec-

ZhangGY2020 (Y uan Zhang, Geng, and Y uan 2020) 
analyzed the Pareto optimization problem of (1|2A| ∑︁

completion time of its jobs, while Agent B seeks the 
minimization of the total weighted late work of jobs 
with common delivery dates. As an NP-hard problem, 
two pseudo-polynomial-time exact algorithms were pro-

LiCT2020 (Shi-Sheng Li, Chen, and Tian 2020) 
explored a dual-agent scheduling problem with a mul-
titasking feature (1|2A, multitasking|f1, f2 ≤ Q). In this 
model, while a task is being executed, it may be inter-
rupted by other unfinished tasks, and the interruption 
time is proportional to the remaining processing time 
of the interrupted task. This characteristic underlines 
the impact of resource contention and job interleaving 
on scheduling performance in a multitasking environ-
ment. Each agent aims to minimise its job completion 
time-oriented objective function, encompassing maxi-
mum regular cost, total completion time, and (weighted) 
number of delayed tasks. The overall optimization objec-
tive is to minimise the other agent’ s objective while lim-
iting one agent’ s objective value to a specified threshold.
FreudM2021 (Freud and Mosheiov 2021) studies a 
single machine dual-agent scheduling problem, where 
one agent aims to minimise total lateness, while the 
other agent must hold its maximum cost within a pre-
set threshold (1|2A| ∑︁ Lj1, f2 ≤ Q). This problem is NP-
hard; a pseudo-polynomial-time dynamic programming 
algorithm was, therefore, developed to solve it. This 
study was further extended to allow for job rejection. 
The authors could solve the problem, for medium-sized 
instances, within a reasonable timeframe.
The article of Y uW2021 (Shaohua Y u and Wu 2021) 
focused on a multi-objective reconciliation model that 
simultaneously incorporates the overall benefits and the 
interests of individual agents. In the model, the work-
shop’ s cost function is minimised first, primarily con-
sidering total completion time and maximum delay, 
ensuring that the other agent’ s fitness function does 
not exceed a threshold (1|2A, workshop objectives|f1 +
f2, f3 ≤ Q). The authors also contributed two polyno-
mial and pseudo-polynomial-time algorithms for vari-
ous combined objectives, analyzed their solvability, and 
investigated computational complexity.
LiC2023 (Shi-Sheng Li and Chen 2023) explored 
a single machine dual-agent scheduling problem with 
release time and interruptibility features. The first 
agent’ s objective was a normal sum-form function, 
while the second agent’ s objective consisted of a max-
form formula or a weighted number of delayed jobs, 
exhibiting a typical heterogeneous objective structure 
(1|2A, rj, pmtn|f1, f2 or Pareto set ). The study ana-
lyzed the problem considering (1) a restricted scenario, 
where the first agent’ s objective was optimised subject 
to constraints for the second agent, and (2) a Pareto 
version, aiming to find all Pareto optimal solutions and 
their corresponding schedules. Polynomial and pseudo-
polynomial time solutions were developed for various 
combinations of objective functions.
PhosavanhO2024 (Phosavanh and Oron 2024) stud-
ied a single machine dual-agent scheduling problem, 
where job processing times can vary due to resource 
upgrades or machine maintenance, with the so-called 
‘rate-modifying activity’ (1|2A, rate − modifying activity|
Cmax, Uj, ∑︁ Lj). In this problem, the agents compete 
for the same resource, with the objective of minimiz-
ing delivery-related metrics (i.e. makespan, number of 
delayed jobs, and total late work). They proved that the 
model under the total late jobs objective is binary NP-
hard and proposed a pseudo-polynomial-time dynamic 
programming algorithm to solve it. Other combinations 
of objectives were shown to be solved in polynomial time.
These studies introduced more flexible and variable 
features, such as release time, interruptible scheduling, 
rate adjustment, job rejection, and multi-task interfer-
ence, thereby broadening the modelling scope of two-
agent and multi-agent scheduling beyond their basic for-
mulations. T o address the increased computational com-
plexity associated with these model extensions, approxi-
mation algorithms, pseudo-polynomial-time algorithms, 
and dynamic programming approaches were developed. 
Lastly, Pareto optimization and other problem-specific

6. Concluding remarks and future research
This study employed the MPA and CA methods to 
provide a systematic review of MAS. Given 628 arti-
cles directly related to the topic, the study mapped 
the backbone of scientific developments and identified 
the main research branches emerging from it. W e pro-
vided a structured understanding of the evolution of 
MAS and offered actionable insights for designing MAS 
systems in modern industrial environments, particu-
larly by identifying how different coordination, nego-
tiation, information-sharing, and learning mechanisms 
contribute to system-level scheduling intelligence across 
different operational contexts. Statistical analysis using 
a logistic growth model indicates that research on MAS 
has increased steadily since 2004, with particularly rapid 
growth after 2015. This trend reflects growing interest 
in multi-agent scheduling across application areas such 
as smart manufacturing, energy storage, supply-chain 
management, and resource sharing in cloud-computing 
environments. The main findings are as follows.
Considered collectively, the evidence reveals a broader 
transformation in the MAS domain: the field is evolv-
ing from viewing multiple agents primarily as competing 
mathematical entities toward treating them as interacting 
decision-making entities capable of coordination, nego-
tiation, adaptation, information sharing, and collective 
learning. Early two-agent scheduling research established 
the mathematical foundations for reconciling compet-
ing objectives under shared resources, but collabora-
tive intelligence becomes increasingly explicit as MAS 
research moves toward dynamic manufacturing, energy 
management, edge computing, and decentralised project 
scheduling. Across these settings, the central challenge 
is no longer simply to optimise multiple agent-specific 
objectives; it is to design mechanisms through which 
distributed agents can exchange information, reconcile 
conflicts, adapt to environmental changes, and gener-
ate system-level scheduling performance. W e therefore 
view the emerging frontier of MAS as a transition from 
multi-agent optimization toward interaction-driven col-
laborative scheduling intelligence.
Analysis of the development paths revealed that the 
knowledge transfer in MAS has evolved from sin-
gle machine and dual-agent models with a focus on 
investigating computational complexity of the problems 
(2006–2012), modelling enrichment for production envi-
ronments with flow shops (2012–2017), advanced com-
putational methods and multi-objective optimization 
(2017–2020), and, most recently, application-oriented 
MAS (2020-now). This trajectory reflects not merely 
an expansion of scheduling models and solution tech-
niques, but a progressive shift in the role assigned to 
agents – from passive carriers of competing objectives to 
autonomous decision-making entities whose interactions 
increasingly determine system-level scheduling perfor-
mance. Additionally, two major branches emerged along 
the main development: (1) inter-agent strategic interac-
tions and game-theoretic behaviour in scheduling; (2) 
including artificial intelligence methods, such as deep 
reinforcement learning and evolutionary algorithms for 
autonomous decision-making with dynamic adaptabil-
ity. Conceptually, these two branches represent comple-
mentary dimensions of the field’ s evolution: the former 
formalises how autonomous agents interact strategically

under conflicting interests, whereas the latter enhances 
their capacity to learn, adapt, and make decentralised 
decisions in dynamic environments. T ogether, they indi-
cate that the development of MAS is increasingly shaped 
by both interaction mechanisms and adaptive intelli-
gence rather than by optimization performance alone.
Analysis of the keywords discovered five major focus 
areas: Cluster I: Dual-agent scheduling in generic single 
machine environments. Cluster II: MAS for dynamic pro-
duction planning. Cluster III: MAS for energy manage-
ment systems. Cluster IV: Resource allocation to agents in 
smart edge systems (edge computing). Cluster V: Negotia-
tion mechanisms in decentralised multi-project schedul-
ing problems. Viewed collectively, these clusters indicate 
that collaborative intelligence is not a single schedul-
ing technique but a family of interaction mechanisms 
whose form depends on the operational context. It ranges 
from fairness-oriented objective reconciliation in generic 
two-agent scheduling, through adaptive decentralised 
coordination in manufacturing and collaborative learn-
ing in energy systems, to real-time distributed resource 
coordination in edge computing and negotiation-driven 
conflict resolution in multi-project environments. The 
common denominator is that scheduling performance 
increasingly emerges from interactions among agents 
rather than from the isolated optimization of individual 
objectives.
From a production-system design and managerial 
perspective, this synthesis further implies that the value 
of MAS does not arise merely from decomposing a 
scheduling problem among multiple agents. Effective 
MAS design requires explicit consideration of how agents 
communicate, what information they share, how con-
flicts are reconciled, how decisions adapt to disruptions, 
and how local actions contribute to system-level objec-
tives. Accordingly, the choice of coordination architec-
ture should be aligned with the dominant operational 
challenge: fairness mechanisms for competing objec-
tives, negotiation and adaptive rescheduling for dynamic 
manufacturing, collaborative learning for energy coordi-
nation, distributed resource-allocation mechanisms for 
edge environments, and negotiation-based protocols for 
decentralised project systems.
On this basis, several directions for future research 
were suggested, the most important of which are sum-
marised as follows.
(1) Existing studies predominantly focus on static 
scheduling and generally assume that planning is 
conducted under complete-information environ-
ments. Future MAS models should accommodate 
dynamic task arrivals, resource interruptions, and 
demand fluctuations by integrating real-time data 
processing and adaptive rescheduling mechanisms 
to provide intelligent decision support in smart fac-
tory and smart energy market environments. An 
important future research question concerns how 
decentralised agents can learn adaptive rescheduling 
policies under incomplete and time-varying infor-
mation environments while simultaneously main-
taining coordination stability, decision consistency, 
and computational scalability.
(2) The operational effectiveness of complex multi-
agent systems relies heavily on self-learning and 
decentralised decision-making capabilities. Deep 
reinforcement learning, fuzzy-control mechanisms, 
and reinforcement-game strategies should be
systematically integrated to achieve adaptive
decision-making and cross-agent coordination in 
decentralised scheduling systems. Future method-
ological developments may further explore graph 
neural networks, multi-agent proximal policy opti-
mization, and explainable reinforcement learning 
frameworks to improve scalability, interpretability, 
coordination transparency, and decision trustwor-
thiness in decentralised scheduling environments.
(3) Real-time simulation and system-prediction capa-
bilities should be systematically integrated with 
MAS models to facilitate simulation-based veri-
fication and optimization for digital-twin appli-
cations. Future studies may further investigate 
hybrid simulation–optimization architectures that 
integrate digital twins, predictive analytics, and rein-
forcement learning mechanisms to support proac-
tive rescheduling, adaptive decision-making, and 
autonomous operational control in Industry 5.0 sys-
tems.
(4) Considering the global trend toward carbon-credit 
mechanisms and low-carbon energy management, 
energy load balancing and charge–discharge plan-
ning for energy-storage systems are expected to 
receive growing research attention. In sustain-
able and low-carbon operational systems, future 
MAS studies may further explore carbon-aware 
scheduling strategies, renewable-energy coordina-
tion mechanisms, and energy-storage-integrated 
production systems. Methodologically, integrating 
graph-based reinforcement learning and distributed 
optimization frameworks may enable more scalable 
and adaptive coordination among heterogeneous 
agents operating across interconnected energy and 
manufacturing infrastructures.
(5) Another promising research direction involves
human–AI collaborative scheduling, where human 
operators and intelligent agents jointly participate

in decentralised and adaptive decision-making pro-
cesses. Future research may further investigate how 
human preferences, fairness considerations, and 
explainability requirements can be systematically 
incorporated into MAS coordination mechanisms 
without significantly compromising optimization 
efficiency and coordination performance.
In addition, building upon the mapping of MAS 
evolution (see Figure 4), a structured research agenda 
can be derived to guide future developments in the 
field. First, following the principle of progressive real-
ism, future studies should incorporate more dynamic and 
uncertain features, such as stochastic job arrivals, real-
time disruptions, and adaptive resource availability, to 
better reflect real-world operational environments. Sec-
ond, under the conflict-coordination trade-off principle, 
future research should develop more advanced multi-
objective and game-theoretic models that explicitly cap-
ture negotiation, cooperation, and competition dynam-
ics among heterogeneous agents. Third, in line with 
the complexity-driven methodological transition, future 
research should further integrate artificial intelligence 
techniques – such as deep reinforcement learning, graph 
neural networks, and hybrid metaheuristics – to effi-
ciently solve large-scale and computationally intractable 
MAS problems. Finally, consistent with the theory-to-
application transition principle, greater emphasis should 
be placed on deploying MAS models in real-world sys-
tems, including smart manufacturing, energy systems, 
and cloud/edge computing, with particular attention to 
scalability, interpretability, and real-time decision sup-
port.
Overall, the findings suggest that MAS should be 
viewed not simply as a means of sequencing com-
peting jobs, but increasingly as a system-level capa-
bility for coordinating decentralised operations. From 
this perspective, collaborative intelligence emerges when 
autonomous or semi-autonomous agents interact
through information sharing, negotiation, coordination, 
adaptation, and collective learning under shared resource 
constraints. Its operational value lies in reconciling local 
objectives with system-level performance across dynamic 
production and service environments.
This research agenda provides a systematic roadmap 
for advancing both the theoretical and practical fron-
tiers of MAS. More fundamentally, these directions sug-
gest that the next stage of MAS research should move 
beyond increasingly sophisticated optimization algo-
rithms toward the development of interaction-driven 
collaborative scheduling systems that integrate decen-
tralised decision making with adaptive intelligence and 
system-level performance. This review is limited to 
papers indexed by W oS, and the analysis considers arti-
cles published until early 2025. Although it demon-
strates high academic quality and representativeness, it 
may overlook some high-quality research from other 
databases, such as Scopus and IEEE. Path analysis of pub-
lished articles and patents indexed by Google Scholar 
may provide a more comprehensive perspective for 
practitioners. Furthermore, MPA and CA rely on the 
construction of citation relationships and keyword co-
occurrence relationships, respectively. Moreover, because 
MPA is based on citation linkages, very recent stud-
ies may be underrepresented owing to the temporal lag 
of citation network formation. Other types of literature 
reviews may deepen our understanding of theoretical 
viewpoints and cross-domain applications of methodolo-
gies. In addition to the five dominant research streams 
identified in this review, the smaller research clusters 
revealed by the CA method deserve continued atten-
tion because they may represent emerging interdisci-
plinary research areas at an early stage of knowledge 
diffusion. Unlike the five dominant clusters, which rep-
resent mature and well-established research streams that 
define the current knowledge structure of the field, these 
smaller clusters capture the initial stages of knowledge 
development and therefore provide valuable early sig-
nals of future research directions, interdisciplinary con-
vergence, and potential paradigm shifts. As collabo-
rative intelligence continues to evolve, these currently 
fragmented research areas may gradually mature into 
major research streams and potentially reshape future 
scheduling paradigms. Therefore, continuous sciento-
metric monitoring of these emerging clusters will be 
valuable for tracking the future evolution of collabora-
tive intelligence, identifying emerging research opportu-
nities, and providing a systematic basis for understand-
ing how emerging collaborative-intelligence technolo-
gies progressively diffuse into mainstream multi-agent 
scheduling research. Finally, CA was employed to pro-
vide a basis for an objective review of academic litera-
ture. Constructing an open-access platform to visualise 
the knowledge graph and provide a semantic search tool 
based on Natural Language Processing (NLP) may help 
provide more actionable suggestions. This will help draw 
the big picture across different fields of study and iden-
tify hotspots where new researchers can more objec-
tively contribute to the development of that field. As 
the final notable limitation of the research method, the 
logistic growth model was used to predict future trends; 
despite the S-curve’ s merits for estimating trendlines, 
the model cannot predict the shifts caused by disruptive 
events and game-changing innovations. Machine learn-
ing algorithms can come in handy to partially address this 
limitation.
