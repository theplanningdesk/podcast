Abstract. In the context of the deep integration of the digital economy, supply chain management is 
confronted with challenges such as demand fluctuations and information asymmetry. Big data 
technology is emerging as the core driving force for the precise transformation of supply chains. This 
paper takes JD Logistics as the research object and systematically analyzes the application paths 
and optimization effects of big data in the e -commerce supply chain. The research starts from the 
characteristics of supply chain big data, summarizes the development process and technical 
architecture of JD Logistics, and focuses on the specific practices of big data in inventory 
management, intelligent warehousing operations, and delivery optimization. Through a combination 
of case analysis and literature research, it demonstrates the significant role of big data technology 
in reducing JD’s warehousing costs and improving delivery efficiency, with a single delivery cost 
decreasing by 8.5%, unit warehousing cost decreasing by 12%, and order fulfillment rate increasing 
to 97%. The study shows that the supply chain management driven by big data achieves the unity 
of cost reduction, efficiency improvement, and service quality enhancement, providing a feasible 
practical path for the digital transformation of e-commerce logistics enterprises. 
Keywords: Big data; Supply chain optimization; JD Logistics; Warehouse management; Inventory 
optimization. 
1. Introduction 
In the era of deep integration of the digital economy in 2026, the global supply chain is undergoing 
unprecedented complexity and dynamic changes. Affected by factors such as geopolitical fluctuations, 
frequent emergencies, and individualized consumer demands, the uncertainty of the supply chain has 
significantly increased. The traditional supply chain management model relies on historical 
experience and static rules, often having the problem of information asymmetry, resulting in demand 
forecasting errors, excessive inventory accumulation and shortage risks coexisting, and being unable 
to adapt to the rapidly changing market environment. Against this backdrop, new -generation 
information technologies represented by big data and artificial intelligence are re configuring the 
operational logic of the supply chain from the bottom layer. 
The comprehensive application of big data technology is fundamentally addressing these pain 
points. By collecting, integrating and analyzing massive multi-dimensional data, enterprises can make 
data-driven, precise decisions in areas such as demand forecas ting, inventory management, route 
optimization and risk assessment, promoting the evolution of supply chain management towards more 
precise, refined and shared directions [1]. For e -commerce logistics enterprises that prioritize speed 
and efficiency as the ir core competitiveness, relying on big data technology to systematically 
restructure traditional processes is not only a technological upgrade but also a strategic choice for 
enterprises to enhance response speed, reduce operating costs and strengthen supply chain resilience 
[2]. The e -commerce logistics industry has entered a new competitive stage of “warehouse and 
distribution integration and technology-driven”, and data elements are becoming the core assets of 
enterprises. 
The academic community has made certain progress in the interdisciplinary research on big data 
and supply chain management. Jiang Qiping systematically reviewed the logic and transformation 
path of the modernization of JD ’s supply chain and pointed out the crucial driving role of data

elements in the reconfiguration of the supply chain [3]. Jiang Pengcheng analyzed the optimization 
path of supply chain management under the big data environment from the perspective of smart 
logistics, emphasizing the application value of deep learning models such as LSTM in demand 
forecasting [4]. Wu using JD as an example, explored the specific practices of big data in procurement, 
warehousing, and distribution from the perspective of cost control [5]. However, most of the existing 
studies have conducted analyses from a single dimension and lack a systematic demonstration of the 
causal relationship between the application of big data at the enterprise level and the optimization 
effect of the supply chain. In particular, the optimization mechanism of the core node of the supply 
chain, namely the warehou sing process, has been insufficiently explored. While big data improves 
efficiency and thereby increases enterprise profits, it is necessary to ask: After actually applying big 
data to the enterprise supply chain, what are the specific optimization mechanisms and paths? 
This paper takes JD Logistics as the case study object, aiming to systematically analyze the 
application status and optimization effect of big data technology in supply chain management, with a 
focus on three dimensions: inventory management in the warehou sing link, intelligent operations, 
and key technologies. The research integrates JD ’s publicly available operational data and existing 
academic achievements, striving to reveal the internal mechanism of how big data drives supply chain 
optimization, and providing theoretical references and practical lessons for the digital transformation 
of e-commerce logistics enterprises. 
2. Characteristics of Supply Chain Big Data 
Firstly, taking JD as an example, JD not only possesses a large amount of customer transaction 
data, but also acquires information such as the total warehouse capacity, delivery locations, user 
information, and even credit information related to JD Finance. 
At the same time, this series of data information was aggregated and formed a diverse and 
comprehensive large database. Then, the customers ’ locations and the merchants ’ inventories were 
also included in this information and applied to the actual supply chain of JD. This can help JD save 
time and costs in the process of planning freight and delivery and other distribution operations, and 
in terms of inventory, it can also help JD make timely responses in inventory management. In 
summary, in the process of optimizing and adjusting the supply chain of JD, big data provides a large 
amount of information and efficiently saves costs. 
Secondly, this big data is updated in real time. On the JD deliveryman APP and the real -time 
monitoring system of the warehouse, JD integrates the obtained freight route data and warehouse 
monitoring data in real time. Therefore, the latest data is benefic ial for planning delivery routes and 
adjusting inventory capacity. The method by which JD obtains the optimal routes and inventory plans 
is to categorize the data and reanalyze these data. In addition, JD will adjust the big data model in 
response to real-time dynamic changes in the data. For example, the demand for various products will 
significantly increase during e -commerce activities. To meet the demands of different market 
situations, JD will analyze the data situation in real time and optimize it and then adjust the resource 
allocation mode. 
Overall, this data can provide a strong guarantee for the supply chain operation of JD. Besides 
being applied in logistics transportation routes and warehouse monitoring, JD can also use big data 
to profile and predict customers, simulate the direction of users' demand s, and thus pre-position the 
goods needed by target users in the nearest warehouse. This approach not only saves time and costs 
on logistics but also helps optimize the management of warehouse inventory. For example, JD has 
established “Asia One” intelligent warehouses and regional distribution centers in various regions as 
its storage facilities.

3. Current Situation of JD Logistics System and Big Data Application 
3.1. The Development History and Scale of JD Logistics 
JD Logistics was established in 2007. Initially, it was the internal logistics department of JD Mall, 
responsible for the warehousing and delivery services of its own products. 
After years of development, JD Logistics has become the leading comprehensive logistics service 
provider in the country. In 2021, JD Logistics was listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. By the 
end of 2024, JD Logistics had over 3,600 warehouses, with a to tal storage area of over 32 million 
square meters, and more than 190,000 delivery personnel. Its services are available in all regions 
across the country. In 2024, JD Logistics ’ annual revenue reached 1.1588 trillion yuan, and in the 
first quarter of 2025, its revenue was 301.1 billion yuan, with a year -on-year growth of 15.8%. The 
reason why JD Logistics can develop rapidly and sustainably over the long term is that it has 
continuously invested in big data and intelligent technologies and has successfully transformed from 
“enterprise logistics” to “logistics enterprise”. 
3.2. JD Logistics Technology Framework 
JD Logistics takes the big data platform as its core and has established a complete technical chain 
from data collection to data storage, data analysis, and finally to intelligent decision -making [6]. In 
terms of data collection, JD collects order informat ion, warehouse status, delivery trajectories, and 
user behavior data in real time through terminal devices such as delivery personnel apps, warehouse 
sensors, RFID tags, and GIS geographic positioning systems. The daily processed data volume 
reaches the pe tabyte level. In terms of data storage and computing, JD relies on its independently 
developed distributed storage system and cloud computing platform to achieve efficient management 
and rapid retrieval of massive heterogeneous data. In terms of intelligent decision-making, JD applies 
various core algorithms, including LSTM demand prediction models, dynamic path optimization 
algorithms, AGV intelligent scheduling systems, and C2M consumer -to-manufacturing direct 
connection platforms, to provide real-time decision support for all links of the supply chain [7]. This 
complete data technology model supports the efficient operation of JD Logistics. 
3.3. JD’s Warehousing Model 
JD’s warehousing system adopts a three -level network architecture of “Regional Distribution 
Center (RDC) - Front-End Distribution Center (FDC) - End-Point Distribution Station”. Among them, 
super-large intelligent warehousing centers represented by “Asia One” are equipped with automated 
sorting systems, AGV (Automated Guided Vehicle) handling robots and intelligent stackers. 
JD can handle millions of orders every day. Using provincial capital cities as regional distribution 
logistics hub centers, they are responsible for the distribution and adjustment of goods within their 
respective regions. For the front-end warehouses, they are deeply located in urban communities and 
county-level markets. Based on big data demand predictions, they prepare goods in advance and 
allocate them to the storage nodes closest to consumers, thereby significantly shortening the delivery 
time [8]. This three-level storage network is deeply integrated with the big data demand prediction 
model, achieving industry-leading levels. The significant achievement is that over 90% of orders are 
delivered within 24 hours [3], providing a solid hardware guarantee for JD’s end delivery timeliness 
and user experience. 
4. Analysis of the Optimization Effect of Big Data on the Warehousing Process 
of JD 
4.1. Inventory Management Optimization 
Through big data analysis, JD ’s inventory management approach has shifted from being 
“experience-driven” to “data-driven”. Traditional inventory management relied on manual 
 
34 
experience to formulate replenishment plans, often resulting in structural contradictions such as 
overstocking of unsold goods and shortages of popular goods. JD adopted the LSTM neural network 
model and combined multi-dimensional data such as users ’ historical purchase behaviors, browsing 
records, and delivery addresses to accurately predict future commodity demands in different regions, 
thereby pre-storing goods at the nearest warehouse nodes to users [9]. This “on-demand storage ” 
proactive replenishment model significantly reduced inventory holding costs, reducing JD ’s 
inventory turnover days to 14 days, which is much lower than the average 30 to 45 days cycle of 
traditional retail enterprises [4]. At the same time, JD established a dynamic replenishment 
mechanism based on real-time sales data, such as when the inventory of a certain SKU in a warehouse 
falls below the safety threshold, the system will automatically generate replenishment instructions to 
ensure that high-traffic goods are prioritized for shipment and to supplement the shortage of goods in 
advance through closed-loop management. Moreover, JD established a supplier evaluation index 
system during the procurement process, using data to evaluate supplier quality, selecting suppliers 
with lower prices while maintaining the same quality, and formulating procurem ent plans based on 
historical sales data and market trends, thereby reducing inventory costs in the upstream of the supply 
chain [5]. 
4.2. Intelligent Warehouse Operation Optimization 
JD has significantly enhanced the operational efficiency of warehouse storage through the deep 
integration of big data and automated equipment. In the case of the “Asia One” intelligent warehouse, 
JD adjusted the AGV robot scheduling system based on operational research algorithms. By solving 
large-scale integer programming problems in real time, it optimized the transportation routes and task 
allocation of the robots, signif icantly improving the turnover efficiency of goods in the warehouse 
[8]. Moreover, JD plans out different outbound tasks based on the spatial and temporal distribution 
characteristics of warehouse orders. After integrating orders with the same or similar d estinations, 
the relevant operators can reduce the distance of their activities and avoid the same and repetitive 
labor when inspecting the goods. In the logistics distribution process, JD combines over 1 billion 
merchant address data, geographic information system (GIS) positioning system, and real-time traffic 
data to calculate the optimal delivery route, thereby reducing the single -item delivery cost [5]. The 
data shows that in the operation of the Asia One warehouse, the single -item delivery cost decreased 
by 8.5%, and the unit warehouse cost decreased by 12% [6]. In terms of order fulfillment efficiency, 
the average fulfillment cycle was shortened from 5.8 days to 3.9 days, the fulfillment efficiency 
increased by approximately 32.8%, and the order fulfi llment rate was raised to 97% [4]. These data 
demonstrate the significant optimization of operational efficiency in various aspects of warehouse 
operations through big data technology. 
4.3. Key Technologies of Warehouse Big Data Analysis 
The key technologies for big data analysis in JD’s warehousing mainly include the following three 
aspects. 
Firstly, the demand prediction model based on LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory Network). This 
model can capture the time series characteristics of user consumption behavior, combine seasonal 
factors and promotional activity variables, and conduct rolling predictions of regional demand for the 
next 7 to 30 days. 
The prediction accuracy can reach over 90% [9]. By deeply integrating the prediction results with 
the Warehouse Management System (WMS), JD has achieved an automated closed loop of 
“prediction to stocking to allocation to replenishment”. Secondly, the intelligent path planning system 
is based on GIS and real-time traffic data. This system integrates massive address data and real-time 
traffic information and can calculate the optimal delivery path from the warehouse to the user within 
seconds. It comprehensively considers multiple factors such as distance, traffic congestion, and 
weather, providing efficient decision support for end delivery. Thirdly, the use r profiling system is 
based on machine learning. By analyzing users’ search, browsing, collection, and purchase history, a

multi-dimensional user tag system is constructed, providing a decision -making basis for precise 
marketing and pre -stocking in the warehouse, thereby improving customer conversion rate while 
reducing ineffective inventory [10]. 
5. The Comprehensive Effectiveness of Big Data Applications 
After applying big data in areas such as transportation, warehousing, and demand estimation for 
goods, JD has achieved significant optimization in terms of both time and financial investment. The 
most direct result is the rapid growth of the company’s profits. After the deep application of big data 
technology, JD’s annual revenue in 2024 was 1.1588 trillion yuan, and in the first quarter of 2025, it 
reached 301.1 billion yuan, with a year -on-year growth of 15.8% [3]. In terms of cost control, by 
monitoring warehouse data in real time, prioritizing the dispatch of g oods with high liquidity and 
making advance replenishments when there is a shortage, the single -item delivery cost in the Asia 
One warehousing case decreased by 8.5%, and the unit warehousing cost decreased by 12%. 
Using the LSTM model to simulate user profiles in advance and prepare goods, combined with 
geographical data to calculate the optimal path, the inventory turnover days only require 14 days [4]. 
At the infrastructure level, JD ’s total warehouse area exceeds 32 million square meters, and the 
total number of warehouses is over 3,600 [3]. This provides a solid storage guarantee for the supply 
of goods. The number of JD delivery personnel exceeds 190,000 [5], supporting a vast end -to-end 
transportation network. In addition, JD holds over 1 billion merchant address data [5], and the 
delivery timeliness has achieved that more than 90% of orders are delivered within 24 hours [3]. Data 
and manpower are important manifestations of JD ’s hardware capabilities. The combination of the 
two has deeply optimized the entire delivery process, significantly improving the delivery time and 
accuracy. 
From the perspective of the effectiveness of market expansion, with the support of user and 
merchant address data, JD can find the nearest merchants for county -level users, shorten the 
transportation time, and better serve the lower -tier market. The orders in county areas of JD have 
increased by 130% year-on-year. 
By analyzing the address data to find better routes and shorten delivery time, JD successfully 
entered the food delivery market, and the number of food delivery orders exceeded 25 million. Using 
the LSTM model to simulate user profiles and prepare goods in advance for promotion and serving 
new users, JD achieved double-digit growth in active user numbers for six consecutive quarter s [3]. 
These achievements further demonstrate that the application of big data in the supply chain not only 
optimizes JD’s investment in supply chain costs, but also continuously improves JD’s profits, market 
share, and supply efficiency [11]. 
6. Conclusion 
This article takes JD Logistics as an example and systematically analyzes the current application 
status and optimization effect of big data technology in supply chain management. The research 
shows that JD has achieved data-driven precise decision-making in inventory management, intelligent 
warehousing operations, and distribution route optimization through building a technical architecture 
centered on the big data platform. Specifically, demand forecasting based on the LSTM model has 
shortened the inventory turnover days to 14 days. The Asia One intelligent warehouse has achieved 
a cost optimization of 8.5% in single-piece delivery costs and 12% in unit storage costs, and over 90% 
of orders have been delivered within 24 hours. In addition, the user profili ng system empowered by 
big data has supported JD's new business expansion in county -level markets and the food delivery 
sector. The core of the above optimization effects lies in the data elements breaking down the 
information barriers among all links of t he supply chain, achieving dynamic matching between the 
demand side and the supply side, and thereby achieving coordinated optimization of cost reduction 
and efficiency improvement as well as service quality enhancement.

This study also has certain limitations. Firstly, the research is mainly based on a single case of JD, 
and the generalizability of the conclusions needs to be verified through comparative studies involving 
multiple enterprises. Secondly, due to the limitat ion of data availability, some optimization effect 
indicators are derived from public data of enterprises and secondary literature, lacking the support of 
first-hand empirical data. Future research can further expand in the following directions: Firstly, 
extend the research perspective to the ecological sustainability dimension of the supply chain, 
exploring the application potential of big data in carbon emission monitoring, green packaging 
optimization, and reverse logistics management; Secondly, pay attention to the integration application 
of emerging technologies such as generative artificial intelligence and digital twins in supply chain 
simulation and emergency management; Thirdly, through simulating the real supply chain network 
in e-commerce promotion activities, real-time simulate the impact of logistics and funds, providing 
forward-looking decision support for supply chain risk management, and constructing a more resilient 
and sustainable intelligent supply chain system.
