Abstract 
Contemporary supply chains operate in an environment of unprecedented volatility, 
characterized by geopolitical disruptions, climate-induced uncertainties, shifting customer 
expectations, and increasingly complex global networks. The ability to anticipate, absorb, adapt 
to, and recover from disruptions —commonly conceptualized as supply chain resilience —has 
become a strategic imperative for manufacturing and distribution organizations. This research 
examines the role of SAP Integrated Business Planning (SAP IBP) in enhancing supply chain 
resilience through integrated planning capabilities that span demand forecasting, supply 
planning, inventory optimization, and cross-functional collaboration. Drawing on a 
combination of platform capability analysis, practitioner-oriented documentation, and reported 
organizational outcomes, the study develops a framework that maps specific SAP IBP 
functional modules to the constituent dimensions of supply chain resilience. The analysis 
identifies four primary resilience-enhancing mechanisms enabled by SAP IBP: anticipatory 
demand sensing through AI-based forecasting algorithms, adaptive supply planning via 
constraint-based optimization and response management, buffering capacity through multi-
stage inventory optimization, and enhanced visibility and response through the Supply Chain 
Control Tower with real-time alerting and case management. The research further examines 
organizational case evidence, including Ericsson’s transformation from siloed manual planning 
to a unified digital platform, to illustrate the practical pathways through which SAP IBP 
capabilities translate into resilience outcomes. The findings suggest that SAP IBP contributes 
to resilience not merely through individual functional enhancements but through the integration 
of planning processes into a coherent, data-consistent, and simulation-capable planning 
environment. The study concludes with implications for practitioners and identifies areas for 
future empirical validation, including the need for longitudinal studies measuring resilience 
outcomes before and after SAP IBP adoption and comparative analyses across organizational 
contexts. 
Keywords: SAP Integrated Business Planning, supply chain resilience, demand forecasting, 
inventory optimization, supply chain control tower, scenario planning, supply chain disruption, 
sales and operations planning 
 
1. Introduction 
Contemporary supply chains operate in an environment characterized by persistent volatility 
and systemic disruption. Geopolitical tensions, climate-related events, pandemic-induced 
demand shocks, and logistical bottlenecks have collectively reshaped the risk landscape within 
which manufacturing and distribution organizations must plan and execute their operations. 
The capacity to anticipate disruptions, absorb their impacts, adapt plans in response, and 
recover efficiently has become a strategic imperative rather than an operational luxury. This 
capacity is commonly conceptualized as supply chain resilience, and it has emerged as a central 
concern in both academic research and practitioner discourse over the past two decades (Shi et 
al., 2024). 
Supply chain resilience is not a singular attribute but a composite of capabilities that span 
temporal phases of disruption management. The literature distinguishes between anticipatory 
capabilities, which enable organizations to detect weak signals and prepare for potential 
disruptions; absorptive capabilities, which allow organizations to withstand shocks without 
significant operational degradation; adaptive capabilities, which facilitate the adjustment of 
plans and processes in response to changing conditions; and restorative capabilities, which 
support recovery and organizational learning following disruption events (Ali et al., 2017). 
These capabilities are enabled by a combination of organizational factors, process maturity, 
and technological infrastructure. 
Enterprise planning systems occupy a central position in the technological infrastructure that 
supports resilience. Among the available platforms, SAP Integrated Business Planning has 
emerged as a significant solution for organizations seeking to integrate demand, supply, 
inventory, and financial planning within a unified cloud-based environment. SAP IBP 
combines capabilities previously distributed across multiple applications, including sales and 
operations planning, demand planning, response and supply planning, inventory optimization, 
and the Supply Chain Control Tower (SAP, 2025a). The platform incorporates advanced 
algorithms, machine learning capabilities, and scenario simulation functions that are directly 
relevant to resilience-enhancing planning practices. 
The research problem addressed in this paper concerns the relationship between SAP IBP 
capabilities and supply chain resilience outcomes. While SAP and its implementation partners 
have documented numerous customer deployments and functional capabilities, the systematic 
articulation of how specific SAP IBP functions contribute to specific resilience dimensions 
remains underdeveloped. Organizations seeking to justify investment in SAP IBP for resilience 
purposes, and practitioners seeking to optimize their use of the platform, would benefit from a 
structured understanding of the mechanisms through which planning capabilities translate into 
resilience outcomes. 
This research is guided by three primary questions. First, what are the constituent capabilities 
of SAP IBP that are relevant to supply chain resilience? Second, through what mechanisms do 
these capabilities enhance the anticipatory, absorptive, adaptive, and restorative dimensions of 
resilience? Third, what evidence exists regarding the practical realization of resilience benefits 
from SAP IBP adoption, and what limitations or contingencies condition these outcomes? 
The significance of this research extends across theoretical and practical domains. 
Theoretically, it contributes to the literature on supply chain resilience by specifying the role 
of integrated planning platforms as resilience-enabling technologies, a perspective that has 
received limited systematic attention. Practically, it provides a structured framework that can 
inform SAP IBP configuration decisions and resilience-focused planning strategies, and it 
identifies areas where further empirical validation is needed. 
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section two reviews relevant literature on 
supply chain resilience and integrated business planning. Section three describes the research 
methodology. Section four presents the results of the capability-resilience mapping analysis. 
Section five discusses the findings, their implications, and limitations. Section six concludes 
with a summary and directions for future research. 
2. Literature Review 
2.1 Supply Chain Resilience: Conceptual Foundations 
Supply chain resilience has been defined and operationalized in multiple ways across the 
operations and supply chain management literature. A systematic review of 103 peer-reviewed 
journal articles identified three major constructs used to define resilience: phases of resilience, 
resilience strategies, and the capabilities needed to be resilient (Ali et al., 2017). Within the 
capabilities construct, five core resilience capabilities emerge: the ability to anticipate, to adapt, 
to respond, to recover, and to learn. These capabilities are supported by thirteen essential 
elements and eighty-four managerial practices that organizations can deploy to enhance their 
resilience posture (Ali et al., 2017). 
The temporal dimension of resilience is critical for understanding how planning systems 
contribute to resilience outcomes. Anticipatory capabilities operate before disruption occurs, 
enabling organizations to model scenarios, identify vulnerabilities, and develop contingency 
plans. Absorptive capabilities operate during the initial impact of disruption, enabling 
organizations to maintain critical operations through buffering mechanisms such as safety stock 
or redundant capacity. Adaptive capabilities operate as disruption persists, enabling 
organizations to reallocate resources, adjust plans, and modify processes. Restorative 
capabilities operate as disruption subsides, enabling organizations to return to normal 
operations and to capture lessons learned for future preparedness. 
Research has also emphasized the role of visibility, flexibility, collaboration, and redundancy 
as resilience-enabling factors. Shi et al. (2024) revisit the Triple-A framework (alignment, 
agility, and adaptiveness), the Triple-P framework (product complexity, process complexity, 
and partnership complexity), and the Triple-R framework (readiness, responsiveness, and 
restoration) to organize the challenges and research gaps in supply chain resilience. Their 
analysis suggests that resilience requires not only the presence of specific capabilities but also 
their integration into coherent organizational processes and technological infrastructures. 
2.2 Integrated Business Planning and SAP IBP 
Integrated business planning refers to the integration of planning processes across functional 
domains—demand, supply, inventory, finance —within a unified planning environment. The 
concept extends traditional sales and operations planning by incorporating financial planning, 
expanding the planning horizon, and leveraging advanced analytics and simulation capabilities. 
Research on IBP implementation has emphasized the importance of digital transformation, 
cross-functional collaboration, and predictive analytics in improving planning efficiency and 
resilience (Abdelghani, 2025). 
SAP Integrated Business Planning is a cloud-based solution that implements the IBP concept 
within the SAP technology stack. The platform organizes its capabilities into several 
application areas, each addressing a distinct planning domain. The Demand application 
supports statistical forecasting, demand sensing, and predictive analytics. The Response and 
Supply application supports constraint-based supply planning, response management, and 
scenario simulation. The Inventory application supports multi-stage inventory optimization and 
safety stock planning. The Sales and Operations application supports the S&OP process, 
including consensus demand planning and supply review. The Supply Chain Control Tower 
provides visibility, alerting, and case management across the end-to-end planning process 
(SAP, 2025a). 
The platform provides supply chain monitoring, sales and operations planning, demand 
management, inventory planning, and supply planning within a comprehensive solution that 
also supports integration with external systems (SAP, 2025a). SAP IBP facilitates the balancing 
of demand and supply, harmonization of planning across corporate functions, responsive 
planning with advanced algorithms and what-if scenario simulation, demand-driven supply 
chain planning based on demand sensing and predictive forecasting, multistage inventory 
optimization, and end-to-end supply chain visibility with real-time monitoring and exception 
management (SAP, 2025a). 
The platform's architecture incorporates artificial intelligence and machine learning 
capabilities that enhance demand forecasting, inventory management, and risk mitigation 
(Immidi and Mane, 2025). Research indicates that AI integration with SAP IBP produces 
substantial reductions in forecast error and inventory carrying costs, while improving risk 
mitigation for supply chain disruptions (Immidi and Mane, 2025). A comparative study of SAP 
IBP and custom AI planning engines found that the platform demonstrates distinct performance 
characteristics under conditions of market volatility and planning assumption breakdown 
(Soundarapandian, 2026). 
2.3 Demand Sensing and Forecasting Capabilities 
Demand sensing represents a critical capability for anticipatory resilience. Unlike traditional 
statistical forecasting, which relies on historical sales patterns, demand sensing uses real-time 
data from point-of-sale systems, social media, weather data, and other sources to detect short-
term demand changes (Lu, 2025). The integration of demand sensing with statistical models 
provides a baseline forecast that is then adjusted based on real-time signals, producing more 
accurate short-term predictions. 
Research on SAP IBP's demand sensing and statistical model capabilities demonstrates 
significant improvements in forecasting accuracy. A case study found that the integration of 
demand sensing with statistical models reduced the root mean square error of demand forecasts 
by twenty percent compared to traditional forecasting methods, while the correlation 
coefficient between forecasted demand and actual sales data increased from 0.7 to 0.9 (Lu, 
2025). These improvements in forecast accuracy translate directly into improved anticipatory 
resilience by enabling organizations to detect demand shifts earlier and prepare more 
effectively. 
The demand sensing process involves data collection from multiple sources, preprocessing to 
remove noise and outliers, and pattern recognition using machine learning algorithms such as 
neural networks and decision trees (Lu, 2025). The statistical model component analyzes 
historical sales data to identify long-term trends and seasonality patterns, with model selection 
based on data characteristics and model fitting using optimization techniques to minimize 
forecasting error (Lu, 2025). The integration of these two approaches provides a more robust 
forecasting capability than either approach alone. 
Sankaran et al. (2019) explore how demand sensing optimizes monthly consensus forecasts 
with short-term sensed demand in daily buckets. They highlight that this optimization method 
is particularly useful for products that are continuously sold and for supply chain environments 
capable of realizing benefits from more accurate short-term predictions. Demand sensing 
addresses variability dampening, working capital reduction, lead time compression, and late 
customization (Sankaran et al., 2019). 
2.4 Response and Supply Planning Capabilities 
The Response and Supply application within SAP IBP provides capabilities for constraint-
based supply planning, response management, and scenario simulation. These capabilities are 
directly relevant to adaptive resilience, enabling organizations to evaluate alternative responses 
to supply disruptions and to adjust plans rapidly as conditions change. 
Scenario-based simulation and trade-off analysis represent particularly important capabilities 
for resilience. SAP IBP's what-if scenario tool enables the simulation of external conditions 
such as recessions or regulatory changes, internal policies such as shift changes or outsourcing, 
and system disruptions such as supply interruption (Al-Kindi, 2025). Scenarios can be tested 
on key performance indicators including resource utilization, lead times, profitability, and 
carbon emissions. Comparing multiple scenarios in overlays provides leadership with the 
information required to choose the most resilient path forward (Al-Kindi, 2025). 
The platform's built-in analytics layer enables real-time visualization of trade-offs and 
performance figures, increasing transparency to facilitate executive-level decision-making (Al-
Kindi, 2025). Collaboration features including Microsoft Excel add-ins, notifications, 
workflow approvals, and integrated machine learning functionality transform SAP IBP into a 
platform not only of planning but of strategic alignment of functions. These features are 
particularly valuable when linking long-term capacity planning with corporate financial 
planning, sales strategy, and sustainability objectives (Al-Kindi, 2025). 
Research on long-term capacity optimization within SAP IBP identifies a systematic process 
for configuring the platform to support multi-year planning horizons. This process includes 
horizon definition, time profile creation, data model alignment, cross-functional integration, 
and scenario simulation (Al-Kindi, 2025). The framework emphasizes that strategic capacity 
decisions require inputs from multiple business functions, and SAP IBP supports this 
coordination through its S&OP module with real-time KPI monitoring, version management, 
and scenario comparison (Al-Kindi, 2025). 
2.5 Inventory Optimization Capabilities 
Inventory optimization represents a critical absorptive resilience capability, providing buffer 
stock that can sustain operations during supply interruptions. SAP IBP's Inventory application 
includes multi-stage inventory optimization, safety stock planning, and AI-assisted inventory 
analysis. The global inventory optimization capability determines optimal safety stock levels 
across the supply network, balancing service level targets against inventory carrying costs 
while considering demand and supply uncertainties (SAP, 2025b). 
Research on safety stock optimization within SAP IBP environments reveals both the potential 
and the challenges of implementing systematic inventory optimization. A study of a Swedish 
food manufacturer that implemented SAP IBP's Inventory Optimizer module found that the 
company continued to rely on manual adjustments of safety stock levels based on staff 
experience, despite having the automated capability available (Jonasson and Blomborg, 2024). 
The researchers developed an alternative inventory model that demonstrated a forty-seven 
percent reduction in safety stock levels compared to current levels while meeting service level 
targets (Jonasson and Blomborg, 2024). 
This finding highlights an important contingency in the relationship between SAP IBP 
capabilities and resilience outcomes: the presence of a capability does not guarantee its 
effective use. Organizational trust in system outputs, data quality, and the maturity of planning 
processes all condition whether capabilities translate into outcomes. The Swedish case suggests 
that building confidence in system recommendations through benchmark validation may be a 
necessary step before organizations fully exploit the absorptive resilience potential of inventory 
optimization. 
2.6 Supply Chain Control Tower and Visibility Capabilities 
The Supply Chain Control Tower represents SAP IBP's capability for end-to-end supply chain 
visibility, real-time monitoring, alerting, and exception management (SAP, 2025a). These 
capabilities are particularly relevant to restorative resilience, enabling rapid detection of issues, 
tracking of their resolution, and application of standardized response procedures. 
Research on supply chain control tower implementations demonstrates their potential for 
transforming operational paradigms from reactive firefighting to predictive intervention. A 
case study of Allegiant Air's control tower implementation, built on SAP Business Technology 
Platform and integrating SAP S/4HANA, Ariba Network, and Analytics Cloud, demonstrated 
substantial improvements in procurement cycle efficiency, supplier collaboration quality, and 
operational downtime reduction (Dameruppula, 2026). The control tower enabled proactive 
alerts for shipment delays and material shortages, transforming operational paradigms from 
reactive firefighting to predictive intervention (Dameruppula, 2026). 
The architecture of the control tower solution established unified visibility across procurement, 
logistics, and inventory operations through event-driven automation and machine learning-
powered forecasting capabilities (Dameruppula, 2026). Implementation followed a phased 
agile framework spanning discovery, development, pilot deployment, and performance 
evaluation. Key performance indicators documented improvement, with procurement response 
time reduced from an average of forty-eight hours to twenty-four hours, and operational 
downtime from parts unavailability reduced from one hundred hours per month to seventy 
hours per month (Dameruppula, 2026). 
Research on control tower maturity suggests that organizations progress through stages of 
capability development, from basic visibility through predictive alerting to prescriptive 
recommendations (Dameruppula et al., 2024). More mature stages incorporate machine 
learning and artificial intelligence to develop smart alert capabilities, insights, and 
recommendations that support prioritization tasks and better mitigate possible disruptions. The 
technologies associated with these stages include IoT, cloud computing, blockchain, big data, 
and digital twins in earlier stages, with machine learning and artificial intelligence becoming 
more prominent in advanced stages (Dameruppula et al., 2024). 
2.7 Organizational and Process Contingencies 
The literature on SAP IBP implementation and integrated business planning consistently 
emphasizes that technology capabilities alone are insufficient to produce resilience outcomes. 
Organizational factors including collaboration, data governance, leadership engagement, and 
process standardization condition the translation of platform capabilities into resilience 
benefits. 
A case study of Prometeon Tyre Group's SAP IBP implementation identified pre-IBP 
challenges including fragmented planning, inaccurate forecasts, and inefficient production 
scheduling (Abdelghani, 2025). The implementation of SAP IBP led to greater forecast 
accuracy, reduced inventory costs, enhanced decision-making agility, and improved real-time 
visibility. The research concluded that IBP's transformative potential in strengthening supply 
chain resilience, digital integration, and strategic execution depends on addressing both 
technological and organizational dimensions (Abdelghani, 2025). 
Research on IBP maturity in the retail sector identified poor forecast accuracy, unreliable 
baseline forecasting models, and insufficient data integration as central challenges (Lund 
University, 2025). A persistent blame culture between departments and a lack of financial 
understanding among planners limit cross-functional collaboration and the ability to evaluate 
the financial impacts of planning decisions. Manual and non-standardized supply planning, 
including allocation decisions and capacity management, reduces efficiency and 
responsiveness. Fragmented systems across the supply network create significant data silos, 
require manual data uploads, and limit the effectiveness of planning tools (Lund University, 
2025). 
The research concluded that improving IBP maturity requires a diverse approach addressing 
three main improvement categories: technology and data infrastructure, behavioral and cultural 
shifts, and process standardization (Lund University, 2025). Recommendations included 
prioritizing the global rollout of planning systems, eliminating manual data integration, 
establishing a single source of truth, implementing a zero-bypass policy regarding demand 
plans, and integrating cross-functional processes to automate manual tasks. 
A study of a new S&OP process implementation noted that both managers and employees stand 
to benefit from greater alignment, operational efficiency, and long-term strategic value, and 
that if implemented thoughtfully, the process can lead to a more resilient and agile planning 
environment (Jalonen, 2024). The research acknowledged that the long-term impacts of the 
process and supporting technologies such as SAP IBP remain uncertain and may evolve as the 
organization gains more experience (Jalonen, 2024). 
2.8 Synthesis and Research Gap 
The literature reviewed above reveals several important insights. Supply chain resilience is a 
multi-dimensional construct comprising anticipatory, absorptive, adaptive, and restorative 
capabilities that operate across temporal phases of disruption. Integrated business planning 
platforms, and SAP IBP specifically, provide functional capabilities that map onto these 
resilience dimensions. However, the relationship between platform capabilities and resilience 
outcomes is conditioned by organizational factors including data quality, process maturity, 
cross-functional collaboration, and leadership engagement. 
A gap remains in the literature regarding the systematic articulation of the mechanisms through 
which SAP IBP capabilities translate into resilience outcomes. The existing literature tends to 
document either functional capabilities or implementation experiences, without connecting the 
two through a structured framework that identifies the specific mechanisms of resilience 
enhancement. This research addresses that gap by developing a capability-resilience mapping 
framework and examining the evidence for resilience outcomes from SAP IBP adoption. 
3. Methodology 
3.1 Research Design 
This study adopts a capability-mapping approach combined with qualitative case analysis. The 
capability-mapping component involves the systematic identification of SAP IBP functional 
capabilities and their association with resilience dimensions based on documented platform 
features and practitioner-oriented literature. The case analysis component examines 
organizational evidence of SAP IBP adoption for resilience-related purposes, drawing on 
publicly available customer accounts, implementation reports, and academic case studies. 
The research design is exploratory and theory-building in nature, appropriate for a topic where 
systematic empirical research is limited. The objective is not to test hypotheses but to develop 
a structured framework that can guide future research and inform practice. The design follows 
an abductive logic, moving iteratively between conceptual framing from the resilience 
literature and empirical observation from the capability documentation and case evidence. 
3.2 Data Sources 
The primary data sources for this research are SAP official documentation, including product 
overviews, help documentation, and capability descriptions (SAP, 2025a; SAP, 2025b; SAP, 
2026). These sources provide authoritative descriptions of SAP IBP functional capabilities and 
are used to construct the capability inventory that forms the basis of the mapping framework. 
Secondary sources include academic theses and research papers on SAP IBP implementation 
and integrated business planning (Abdelghani, 2025; Al-Kindi, 2025; Lund University, 2025), 
practitioner-oriented analyses of SAP IBP capabilities (Sankaran et al., 2019; Lu, 2025), and 
published case studies of control tower implementations and supply chain transformations 
(Dameruppula, 2026; Dameruppula et al., 2024). These sources provide context regarding how 
capabilities are applied in practice and what outcomes organizations report. 
The analysis also draws on the conceptual literature on supply chain resilience to define the 
resilience dimensions against which SAP IBP capabilities are mapped. This literature provides 
the theoretical structure that organizes the mapping analysis (Ali et al., 2017; Shi et al., 2024). 
3.3 Analytical Procedure 
The analysis proceeded in three stages. First, a comprehensive inventory of SAP IBP 
capabilities was compiled from the primary documentation sources. Capabilities were 
categorized by application area (Demand, Response and Supply, Inventory, S&OP, Supply 
Chain Control Tower) and by functional type (forecasting, optimization, simulation, visibility, 
collaboration). 
Second, the resilience dimensions were operationalized based on the conceptual literature. Four 
dimensions were selected for analysis: anticipatory, absorptive, adaptive, and restorative. 
These dimensions were selected because they correspond to distinct temporal phases of 
disruption management and because they provide a parsimonious yet comprehensive structure 
for organizing resilience capabilities (Ali et al., 2017). 
Third, a mapping matrix was constructed associating each SAP IBP capability with one or more 
resilience dimensions. The mapping was based on the documented function of each capability 
and its logical relationship to resilience outcomes. For example, AI-based demand forecasting 
was mapped to anticipatory resilience because improved forecast accuracy enables earlier 
detection of demand shifts and more effective preparation. 
3.4 Case Selection and Analysis 
Case evidence was selected based on the availability of documented SAP IBP or control tower 
implementations with reported outcomes relevant to resilience. The cases examined include 
Ericsson's supply chain transformation, Prometeon Tyre Group's SAP IBP implementation, 
Allegiant Air's control tower deployment, and a Swedish food manufacturer's inventory 
optimization experience. These cases were selected to provide diversity in industry context, 
implementation scope, and resilience dimension emphasis. 
Case analysis involved extracting reported outcomes, implementation challenges, and 
organizational contingencies from the available documentation. The analysis focused on 
identifying how specific capabilities were used, what outcomes were observed, and what 
factors conditioned the realization of resilience benefits. 
3.5 Limitations 
The methodology has limitations that should be acknowledged. The reliance on documented 
capabilities and publicly available case evidence rather than primary empirical data limits the 
depth of the findings. The mapping framework is conceptual and has not been validated through 
systematic empirical testing. The case evidence is limited to a small number of organizations 
and may emphasize positive outcomes due to publication bias. 
4. Results 
4.1 Capability Inventory 
The analysis identified five primary application areas within SAP IBP, each containing specific 
capabilities relevant to resilience. The Demand application includes statistical forecasting, 
demand sensing, predictive analytics, and forecast consumption management. The Response 
and Supply application includes constraint-based supply planning, response management, 
source-of-supply modeling, and what-if scenario simulation. The Inventory application 
includes multi-stage inventory optimization, safety stock planning, service level optimization, 
and AI-assisted inventory analysis. The Sales and Operations application includes consensus 
demand planning, supply review, and financial integration. The Supply Chain Control Tower 
includes custom alerts, case management, procedure playbooks, and intelligent visibility (SAP, 
2025a). 
4.2 Resilience Dimension Mapping 
The mapping analysis revealed distinct patterns of capability-dimension association. 
For anticipatory resilience, the capabilities with the strongest associations are demand sensing, 
AI-based forecasting, scenario simulation, and predictive analytics. Demand sensing enables 
the detection of short-term demand shifts that may signal emerging disruptions. The integration 
of demand sensing with statistical models provides a baseline forecast that is adjusted based 
on real-time data, improving accuracy by twenty percent in reported cases (Lu, 2025). AI-based 
forecasting improves the accuracy of baseline demand predictions through machine learning 
algorithms that identify patterns and relationships not captured by traditional statistical 
methods (Immidi and Mane, 2025). Scenario simulation allows planners to model potential 
disruptions and develop contingency plans before disruptions occur (Al-Kindi, 2025). 
For absorptive resilience, the capabilities with the strongest associations are multi-stage 
inventory optimization, safety stock planning, and strategic inventory buffering. Multi-stage 
inventory optimization determines optimal safety stock levels across the supply network, 
balancing service level targets against inventory carrying costs while considering demand and 
supply uncertainties (SAP, 2025b). The AI-assisted inventory analysis capability supports this 
by identifying the key drivers of safety stock values, enabling more informed inventory policy 
decisions. Research demonstrates that systematic safety stock optimization can reduce 
inventory levels by up to forty-seven percent while maintaining service level targets (Jonasson 
and Blomborg, 2024). 
For adaptive resilience, the capabilities with the strongest associations are response 
management, source-of-supply modeling, and what-if simulation. Response management 
enables planners to evaluate alternative responses to supply disruptions, such as sourcing from 
alternative suppliers or reallocating inventory across locations. Source-of-supply modeling 
allows the representation of multiple sourcing options with different lead times, capacities, and 
costs, so that alternative sourcing scenarios can be rapidly evaluated during disruptions. What-
if simulation supports adaptive response by allowing planners to compare the outcomes of 
different response strategies before committing to one (Al-Kindi, 2025). 
For restorative resilience, the capabilities with the strongest associations are the Supply Chain 
Control Tower's alerting, case management, and procedure playbooks. Custom alerts identify 
critical supply chain issues such as inventory shortages, supply-demand imbalances, or 
unexpected changes. Case management facilitates the tracking and resolution of these issues. 
Procedure playbooks provide standardized guidance for resolving specific types of supply 
chain problems, enabling faster and more consistent recovery. The integration of financial 
planning with operational planning through the S&OP application also supports restorative 
resilience by enabling rapid assessment of the financial implications of recovery options. 
Research on control tower implementations demonstrates substantial improvements in 
procurement cycle efficiency, supplier collaboration quality, and operational downtime 
reduction (Dameruppula, 2026). 
4.3 Case Evidence 
The case evidence provides illustrative support for the mechanisms through which SAP IBP 
capabilities translate into resilience outcomes. 
Ericsson described its pre-transformation state as characterized by siloed capabilities, 
fragmented processes, lack of a common master data model, and extensive reliance on manual 
spreadsheets. The transformation aimed to integrate demand, supply, inventory, and S&OP into 
a unified platform. Reported expectations included improved demand plan accuracy, better 
control, faster execution, and greater responsiveness to customer demands. 
Prometeon Tyre Group's SAP IBP implementation addressed pre-IBP challenges including 
fragmented planning, inaccurate forecasts, and inefficient production scheduling. The 
implementation led to greater forecast accuracy, reduced inventory costs, enhanced decision-
making agility, and improved real-time visibility (Abdelghani, 2025). The case demonstrates 
the transition from a planning environment with limited anticipatory and adaptive capability to 
one with substantially higher capability. 
Allegiant Air's control tower implementation, built on SAP Business Technology Platform, 
integrated S/4HANA, Ariba Network, and Analytics Cloud to establish unified visibility across 
procurement, logistics, and inventory operations (Dameruppula, 2026). The solution enabled 
proactive alerts for shipment delays and material shortages, transforming operational 
paradigms from reactive firefighting to predictive intervention. Procurement response time was 
reduced by fifty percent, and operational downtime from parts unavailability was reduced by 
thirty percent (Dameruppula, 2026). 
The Swedish food manufacturer case illustrates the contingency between capability availability 
and capability use. Despite implementing SAP IBP's Inventory Optimizer module, the 
company continued to rely on manual adjustments of safety stock levels based on staff 
experience (Jonasson and Blomborg, 2024). This finding highlights that the presence of a 
capability does not guarantee its effective use, and that organizational trust and process 
maturity condition the realization of resilience benefits. 
4.4 Organizational Contingencies 
The case evidence and implementation research consistently identify organizational factors that 
condition the translation of SAP IBP capabilities into resilience outcomes. 
Data quality and integration represent a recurring challenge. Fragmented systems, inconsistent 
master data, and manual data uploads limit the effectiveness of planning tools (Lund 
University, 2025). The establishment of a single source of truth and the elimination of manual 
data integration are identified as critical enablers of planning maturity. 
Cross-functional collaboration and leadership engagement are also critical. A persistent blame 
culture between departments and a lack of financial understanding among planners limit the 
ability to evaluate the financial impacts of planning decisions (Lund University, 2025). 
Recommendations include implementing a zero-bypass policy regarding demand plans, 
establishing an IBP governance council, and investing in training and onboarding programs. 
Process standardization and maturity condition the effectiveness of platform capabilities. 
Research on IBP maturity identifies three improvement categories: technology and data 
infrastructure, behavioral and cultural shifts, and process standardization (Lund University, 
2025). Organizations must address all three to realize the resilience potential of integrated 
business planning. 
5. Discussion of Findings 
5.1 Interpretation of Results 
The mapping analysis demonstrates that SAP IBP capabilities are distributed across all four 
resilience dimensions, with particularly strong coverage of anticipatory and adaptive 
capabilities. This distribution aligns with the platform's design emphasis on advanced analytics, 
simulation, and response management. The capabilities most directly associated with 
absorptive resilience —inventory optimization and safety stock planning —are also well-
developed, reflecting the platform's heritage in inventory planning. 
The findings suggest that SAP IBP contributes to resilience through multiple mechanisms 
operating at different levels of the planning process. At the data level, the unified data model 
and master data consistency checks reduce the risk of planning errors arising from inconsistent 
or outdated information. At the analytical level, AI-based forecasting and optimization 
algorithms improve the quality of planning decisions. At the process level, integration across 
demand, supply, inventory, and S&OP enables coordinated response to disruptions. At the 
visibility level, the Supply Chain Control Tower provides real-time awareness of emerging 
issues and structured processes for their resolution. 
This multi-level mechanism perspective is important because it suggests that the resilience 
benefits of SAP IBP are not attributable to any single capability but rather to the integration of 
capabilities into a coherent planning environment. Organizations that adopt SAP IBP but fail 
to integrate its capabilities —or that use it as a replacement for existing planning systems 
without transforming planning processes —may realize fewer resilience benefits than 
organizations that fully exploit the platform's integration potential. The Swedish food 
manufacturer case provides empirical support for this interpretation, demonstrating that the 
availability of an inventory optimization capability did not automatically lead to its effective 
use (Jonasson and Blomborg, 2024). 
5.2 Relation to Existing Literature 
The findings are broadly consistent with the conceptual literature on supply chain resilience, 
which emphasizes the importance of visibility, flexibility, collaboration, and redundancy as 
resilience-enabling factors. SAP IBP capabilities map onto these factors in the following ways: 
visibility is enabled by the Supply Chain Control Tower and the unified data model; flexibility 
is enabled by scenario simulation and response management; collaboration is enabled by the 
integrated S&OP process and the shared planning environment; and redundancy is enabled by 
inventory optimization and safety stock planning. 
The findings also extend the literature by specifying the technological capabilities through 
which these resilience factors can be operationalized in practice. While the conceptual literature 
identifies what resilience requires (Ali et al., 2017; Shi et al., 2024), the SAP IBP capability 
mapping identifies how a specific technology platform can deliver those requirements. This 
specification is useful for both research and practice: for research, it provides a basis for 
operationalizing resilience constructs in studies of planning technology adoption; for practice, 
it provides a structured basis for capability assessment and investment prioritization. 
The research contributes to the growing literature on AI integration with supply chain planning 
by demonstrating how machine learning capabilities within SAP IBP enhance specific 
resilience dimensions. The literature indicates that AI integration produces substantial 
reductions in forecast error and inventory carrying costs while improving risk mitigation 
(Immidi and Mane, 2025). The mapping framework developed in this research specifies which 
resilience dimensions benefit most from AI capabilities and through what mechanisms. 
5.3 Implications for Practice 
Several practical implications emerge from this research. First, organizations seeking to 
enhance resilience through SAP IBP should adopt a capability-oriented perspective, focusing 
not merely on implementing the platform but on developing the specific capabilities that map 
to their prioritized resilience dimensions. Organizations facing high demand volatility may 
prioritize demand sensing and AI-based forecasting; organizations facing supply network 
fragility may prioritize source-of-supply modeling and multi-stage inventory optimization. 
Second, the integration of capabilities matters more than the capabilities themselves. SAP IBP's 
resilience value proposition rests substantially on the integration of demand, supply, inventory, 
and financial planning within a unified environment. Organizations should design their 
implementation to exploit this integration, avoiding the temptation to replicate pre-existing 
silos within the new platform. 
Third, organizational factors including data quality, cross-functional collaboration, and 
leadership engagement are necessary conditions for realizing resilience benefits. The Swedish 
food manufacturer case demonstrates that capability availability does not guarantee capability 
use. Organizations should invest in data governance, change management, and training 
alongside technology implementation. 
Fourth, the Supply Chain Control Tower capabilities deserve particular attention for restorative 
resilience. The ability to detect issues rapidly, track their resolution, and apply standardized 
procedures is critical for minimizing the duration and impact of disruptions. Organizations 
should invest in configuring alerts that are meaningful and actionable rather than 
overwhelming, and in developing procedure playbooks that codify organizational knowledge 
about disruption response. 
5.4 Limitations and Future Research 
This research has several limitations. The most significant is the reliance on documented 
platform capabilities and illustrative case evidence rather than systematic empirical data on 
resilience outcomes. The mapping framework is conceptual and has not been validated through 
empirical testing. The case evidence is limited to a small number of organizations and is based 
on publicly available materials that may emphasize positive outcomes. 
Future research should address these limitations through empirical studies that measure 
resilience outcomes before and after SAP IBP adoption, using appropriate control groups or 
longitudinal designs. Research should also examine the organizational and process factors that 
moderate the relationship between SAP IBP capabilities and resilience outcomes. Comparative 
studies across organizational contexts —different industries, different sizes, different supply 
chain configurations —would help identify the contingencies that condition the resilience 
benefits of SAP IBP. Finally, research should examine the specific mechanisms through which 
SAP IBP capabilities are used during actual disruptions, including the decision processes and 
organizational routines that translate planning capabilities into response actions. 
6. Conclusion 
This research has examined the relationship between SAP Integrated Business Planning 
capabilities and supply chain resilience. Through a systematic mapping of SAP IBP functional 
capabilities to the anticipatory, absorptive, adaptive, and restorative dimensions of resilience, 
the study has developed a framework that articulates the mechanisms through which the 
platform contributes to resilience outcomes. 
The analysis identified four primary resilience-enhancing mechanisms. Anticipatory resilience 
is enhanced through demand sensing, AI-based forecasting, and scenario simulation, which 
improve the detection of emerging demand shifts and the preparation of contingency plans. 
Absorptive resilience is enhanced through multi-stage inventory optimization and safety stock 
planning, which establish buffer stocks that can sustain operations during supply interruptions. 
Adaptive resilience is enhanced through response management, source-of-supply modeling, 
and what-if simulation, which enable rapid evaluation and selection of response strategies. 
Restorative resilience is enhanced through control tower alerting, case management, and 
procedure playbooks, which facilitate rapid detection and resolution of supply chain issues. 
These mechanisms operate through the integration of planning processes across functional 
domains and through the analytical capabilities embedded in the platform. The research 
contributes to the literature by specifying the technological capabilities through which 
integrated business planning platforms enable supply chain resilience, a connection that has 
been assumed but not systematically articulated. It contributes to practice by providing a 
framework that can inform SAP IBP configuration decisions and resilience-focused planning 
strategies. 
The limitations of the research point toward a program of empirical work that would validate 
and extend the conceptual framework developed here. As supply chains continue to face 
volatile and uncertain conditions, the ability to anticipate disruptions, absorb their impacts, 
adapt plans rapidly, and restore operations efficiently will remain a critical organizational 
capability. Understanding how integrated planning platforms such as SAP IBP contribute to 
this capability is therefore of continuing theoretical and practical importance.
