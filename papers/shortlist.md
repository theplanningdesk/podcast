## 1. Impact of privacy-preserving horizontal retailer information sharing on the bullwhip effect in supply chains with multiple retailers

Martin Hrušovský, Emel Arıkan, Lena Silbermayr. International Journal of Production Economics, 2026-11-02. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: Vienna University of Economics and Business (AT)
Lanes: Inventory. Score 8.0: Nordic/DACH authors (AT); established journal; practice signals: manufacturer, real-world, retailer.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2026.110258 · No full text saved

Experiences from real-world supply chains as well as exhaustive research show that the lack of coordination within supply chains leads to bullwhip effect, in which the magnitude of fluctuations in orders increases in the upstream direction from retailer to the supplier. One potential solution for addressing this phenomenon is information sharing between supply chain actors that can reduce the impact of fluctuations in customer demand on the supply chain. However, sharing too much information might lead to higher vulnerability of stakeholders in negotiations with their partners or to an unwanted leakage of information to competitors. Therefore, relevant actors are often reluctant to share the necessary information. To overcome these problems, we propose a horizontal information sharing model where retailers share demand information in form of average values between each other without revealing the exact values of each individual partner. This could be achieved by, e.g., applying costly cryptographic methods. Therefore, we evaluate the potential effects of this type of information sharing on the whole supply chain by developing a simulation model representing a 4-echelon supply chain, which consists of multiple customers, each served by their own retailer, one wholesaler, one manufacturer and one supplier. We investigate the effect of horizontal information sharing of average customer demand among the retailers on the bullwhip effect and show its effects on the different supply chain actors. In addition, the influences of demand variability and demand correlations between customers, different forecasting methods, safety stock approaches, stochastic lead times and the number of retailers sharing the information on the bullwhip effect are analyzed.

Hospitals face challenges in long-term intensive care unit (ICU) capacity planning under uncertain demand. Admission rates fluctuate over time, and LOS distributions vary with patient heterogeneity, hospital location, case mix, and clinical practice. Common approaches rely on steady-state queueing models or heuristic rules with fixed parameters, which often fail to capture real occupancy dynamics. The widely used 85% occupancy rule, for example, recommends keeping average utilization below this level to preserve responsiveness, yet it is grounded in stationary assumptions and may lack resilience in time-varying systems. Our analysis shows that even when long-run utilization targets are satisfied, daily occupancy often exceeds 100% capacity. We propose a data-driven framework to estimate ICU bed occupancy using an M t / G t / ∞ queueing model with time-varying arrival rates and empirically fitted LOS distributions. The approach combines statistical decomposition and parametric fitting to capture temporal patterns in admissions and LOS, and is applied to multi-year data from neonatal ICUs (NICUs) in Calgary. We evaluate capacity scenarios including average-based thresholds and Poisson-based surge estimates. Results show that static heuristics are inadequate under fluctuating demand and underscore the importance of modeling LOS variability when estimating bed needs. Although the case study focuses on NICUs, the framework has potential applicability to other ICU settings and provides data-informed support for systems facing rising demand and constrained capacity.

## 8. Flamescapes: Simulating the Wildlandfire Urban Interface

Institutions: Christian-Albrechts-Universität zu Kiel; Adam Mickiewicz University in Poznań; King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (DE, PL, SA)
Lanes: Network design. Score 4.7: Nordic/DACH authors (DE); practice signals: real-world.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1145/3842518 · No full text saved

We present a physically based simulation framework for wildland-urban interface (WUI) fires that couples material-aware urban scenes with interactive combustion simulation. Motivated by the dominant WUI exposure pathways of direct flame contact, thermal radiation, and ember transport, our framework combines heterogeneous solid combustion, radiative heat transfer, ember transport, and explicit flame-front propagation. Urban scenes are generated from OpenStreetMap footprints using a procedural pipeline that represents buildings as combustible 3D structures composed of semantic atomic modules with material and fuel annotations. Building on a chemistry-based hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian fire solver, we model material-dependent heating, conduction, pyrolysis, oxidation, fuel depletion, and ember generation. A hierarchical fuel graph couples semantic scene structure to the solver's solid fuel grid, enabling subcomponent-level mesh degradation. We demonstrate structure-to-structure ignition, radiative preheating, material-dependent burning, ember-driven spot fires, and validate the method through ablations and comparisons to real-world combustion experiments.

## 9. Metalog Materials

Hiroaki Sakai, Saip-Can Hasbay, Christian Freude, Michael H. Wimmer, David Hahn. ACM Transactions on Graphics, 2026-11-09. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: TU Wien (AT)
Lanes: Network design. Score 4.7: Nordic/DACH authors (AT); practice signals: real-world.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1145/3842563 · No full text saved

A central aspect of modern physically based rendering is the practical yet realistic modeling of material appearance, which requires efficient and accurate representations of bidirectional reflectance distribution functions (BRDFs). In this paper, we present a unified material reflectance representation based on metalog distributions. Introducing quantile-based metalog interpolation and metalog mixture models, our representation captures a wide range of complex reflectance behaviors of both analytic models and real-world materials, while remaining interpretable and efficient. Our approach achieves competitive runtime performance and a small memory footprint, while enabling continuous interpolation between fundamentally different reflectance behaviors. Furthermore, our model is fully differentiable with respect to the coefficients defining the distributions, thus easily integrates into existing differentiable rendering pipelines. In summary, metalog materials can effectively represent real-world reflectance data, interpolate between multiple materials within a unified representation, and be acquired from real-world images via differentiable rendering.
