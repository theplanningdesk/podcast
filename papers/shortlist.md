## 1. Impact of privacy-preserving horizontal retailer information sharing on the bullwhip effect in supply chains with multiple retailers

Martin Hrušovský, Emel Arıkan, Lena Silbermayr. International Journal of Production Economics, 2026-11-02. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: Vienna University of Economics and Business (AT)
Lanes: Inventory. Score 8.0: Nordic/DACH authors (AT); established journal; practice signals: manufacturer, real-world, retailer.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2026.110258 · No full text saved

Experiences from real-world supply chains as well as exhaustive research show that the lack of coordination within supply chains leads to bullwhip effect, in which the magnitude of fluctuations in orders increases in the upstream direction from retailer to the supplier. One potential solution for addressing this phenomenon is information sharing between supply chain actors that can reduce the impact of fluctuations in customer demand on the supply chain. However, sharing too much information might lead to higher vulnerability of stakeholders in negotiations with their partners or to an unwanted leakage of information to competitors. Therefore, relevant actors are often reluctant to share the necessary information. To overcome these problems, we propose a horizontal information sharing model where retailers share demand information in form of average values between each other without revealing the exact values of each individual partner. This could be achieved by, e.g., applying costly cryptographic methods. Therefore, we evaluate the potential effects of this type of information sharing on the whole supply chain by developing a simulation model representing a 4-echelon supply chain, which consists of multiple customers, each served by their own retailer, one wholesaler, one manufacturer and one supplier. We investigate the effect of horizontal information sharing of average customer demand among the retailers on the bullwhip effect and show its effects on the different supply chain actors. In addition, the influences of demand variability and demand correlations between customers, different forecasting methods, safety stock approaches, stochastic lead times and the number of retailers sharing the information on the bullwhip effect are analyzed.

## 2. Dynamic Aggregate Planning of a Make-to-Order Production System

Valtteri Vironmäki, Joonas Ahola, Esko Niemi. Research Square, 2026-11-06. preprint, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: Aalto University (FI)
Lanes: Production planning and scheduling. Score 4.7: Nordic/DACH authors (FI); preprint, not peer-reviewed; practice signals: real-world.
Link: https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11261258/v1 · Full text: papers/W7225424568.txt

Abstract In Make-to-Order (MTO) production, products and production are non-standard and incoming orders arrive variably, with varying work content and due dates. Aggregate Production Planning (APP) takes place in the medium run, and the purpose of it is to match resource capacity with demand. In APP, orders are roughly scheduled for processing so that the resource capacity constraints and deadlines can be met. The static resource constrained project scheduling problem is the conventional research approach, but real-world production planning requires dynamic scheduling to integrate new orders over time. The dynamic model in this study employs periodic scheduling, treating it as sequential static mixed integer linear optimization problems, focusing on minimizing tardiness and rescheduling disruptions. The model is applied in experiments concerning a Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS) -centered MTO production system. The experimentation focuses on the effects of replanning frequency, order arrival rate, delivery date slack, forward planning vs. backward planning, restrictions in order processing activity timing and activity workload allocation method on the planning results. Based on the results, conclusions and guidance applicable to practical production planning are provided.

## 3. Perspectives on Health Logistics in Saudi Arabia: Progress and Challenges

Haytham A. Sheerah, Meraee Aljabri, Shouq M. Alzaaqi, Emad Lafi Almohammadi, Hebah Dada, Abdulrhman I. Mohana et al.. Healthcare, 2026-11-09. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: King Abdulaziz University; Ministry of Health; King Saud bin Abdulaziz University for Health Sciences; King Abdullah International Medical Research Center; National Guard Health Affairs (DE, SA)
Lanes: Resilience and risk. Score 4.0: Nordic/DACH authors (DE).
Link: https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare14213831 · No full text saved

Saudi Arabia has identified healthcare supply chain transformation as a key component of the Health Sector Transformation Program under Vision 2030, yet evidence on these reforms remains fragmented across sectors and policy domains. Drawing on the published literature, policy documents, institutional reports, and the authors’ experience in national procurement, regulation, and public health, this perspective examines four interacting components of transformation—procurement governance, digital infrastructure, local manufacturing capacity, and emergency preparedness—and applies this framework to antimicrobial resistance. Substantial reforms have been implemented, notably centralized procurement through the National Unified Procurement Company, national digital surveillance and traceability systems, and the localization of strategic medicines such as insulin. We propose that interactions among these reforms may influence supply chain resilience, although weak public–private data integration, dependence on imported active pharmaceutical ingredients, and limited performance evaluations prevent firm conclusions about their system-level effects. Remaining challenges include recurrent medicine shortages, regulatory bottlenecks, workforce gaps, and the limited availability of public performance indicators. The Saudi experience suggests that centralized, state-led reform should balance efficiency with redundancy, prioritize localization selectively, and build transparent performance monitoring into reforms from the outset.

## 4. Optimization of Pharmacy Supply Chains Using AI and Machine Learning

Er. Niharika Singh. International Journal of Medical Research And Innovation in Applied Science (IJMRIAS), 2026-11-03. article, licence cc-by-nc, cited 0.
Institutions: ABES Engineering College (IN)

Link: https://ijmrias.org/index.php/ijmrias/article/view/48 · Full text: papers/W7218063460.txt

Pharmacy supply chains are under intensifying pressure to deliver the right medicine, in the right quantity, at the right time—while minimizing costs, preventing expiries, and complying with stringent regulatory and quality standards. Conventional forecasting and replenishment methods often struggle with volatile demand, multi-echelon dynamics (manufacturer–wholesaler–pharmacy), and constraints such as cold-chain integrity and controlled-substance regulations. This manuscript presents an integrated framework for optimizing pharmacy supply chains using artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML). We synthesize current methods in demand forecasting, inventory control, routing and scheduling, and risk management, and we propose a practical methodology that combines feature-rich forecasting (gradient-boosted trees and sequence models), stochastic inventory optimization, reinforcement learning for adaptive reorder policies, and constraint-aware vehicle routing. A detailed study protocol outlines quasi-experimental deployment across community and hospital pharmacies, including data governance, evaluation metrics, and change-management steps. Illustrative results (based on a realistic implementation scenario) indicate reductions in stockouts (30–50%), expiries (25–40%), and total holding costs (10–20%), with improvements in service level (3–10 percentage points) and forecast accuracy (MAPE reductions from ~22–28% to ~9–14%). We conclude with discussion on interpretability, workforce upskilling, and governance, emphasizing a socio-technical approach that blends human oversight with algorithmic optimization to achieve resilient, ethical, and efficient pharmacy supply chains.

Accurate and calibrated reference evapotranspiration (ET0) forecasts are essential for irri-gation scheduling, yet many ET0 studies report deterministic point forecasts and evaluate only a limited number of forecast origins. This study compared deterministic and proba-bilistic ET0 forecasts at seven NCEI stations in northern and southern Xinjiang, including Kashi, Hotan, Korla and Turpan as external stations. Daily records from 1 January 1984 to 16 May 2025 were divided chronologically into 70% training, 10% validation and 20% testing.
