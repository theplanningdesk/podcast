## 1. Plug and schedule: scalable and modular architecture for integrated and dynamic scheduling based on graph neural networks and reinforcement learning

Yannik Hermann, Vincent Schröttle, Benedikt Kaupp, Martin Benfer, Gisela Lanza. International Journal of Production Research, 2026-10-08. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (DE)
Lanes: Production planning and scheduling, AI in planning. Score 9.5: Nordic/DACH authors (DE); established journal; practice signals: german, industrial, manufacturer; reports a quantified result.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2026.2741380 · No full text saved

Manufacturing companies increasingly require scheduling approaches that integrate practical constraints such as workforce availability, maintenance, setup dependencies, energy objectives, and uncertainty, yet existing research offers limited support for transferring them into heterogeneous industrial data landscapes. This paper proposes a scalable and modular architecture for integrated and dynamic scheduling that combines an Asset Administration Shell-based middleware for standardised data integration, a heterogeneous graph representation of scheduling instances, a graph neural network-based State Modeler, and a Proximal Policy Optimisation-based RL-Scheduler coupled with event-based simulation. The architecture is validated on the production system of a German machinery manufacturer with 59 manufacturing resources and 39 employees and on public benchmark instances. On the industrial instances, the learned policy reduces the makespan against the mean of seven dispatching baselines by 2.93% to 6.76% across six evaluation cells covering small-part, large-part, and combined production under deterministic and stochastic machine failures, with statistical significance in every scenario. The energy-aware configuration preserves this makespan performance while significantly reducing the relative energy cost in most scenarios. The findings indicate that the proposed architecture provides a practically transferable pathway for configurable RL-based scheduling in heterogeneous production environments.

## 2. Impact of privacy-preserving horizontal retailer information sharing on the bullwhip effect in supply chains with multiple retailers

Martin Hrušovský, Emel Arıkan, Lena Silbermayr. International Journal of Production Economics, 2026-11-02. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: Vienna University of Economics and Business (AT)
Lanes: Forecasting, Inventory, Resilience and risk. Score 8.5: Nordic/DACH authors (AT); established journal; practice signals: manufacturer, real-world, retailer.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2026.110258 · Full text: papers/W7225117494.txt

Experiences from real-world supply chains as well as exhaustive research show that the lack of coordination within supply chains leads to bullwhip effect, in which the magnitude of fluctuations in orders increases in the upstream direction from retailer to the supplier. One potential solution for addressing this phenomenon is information sharing between supply chain actors that can reduce the impact of fluctuations in customer demand on the supply chain. However, sharing too much information might lead to higher vulnerability of stakeholders in negotiations with their partners or to an unwanted leakage of information to competitors. Therefore, relevant actors are often reluctant to share the necessary information. To overcome these problems, we propose a horizontal information sharing model where retailers share demand information in form of average values between each other without revealing the exact values of each individual partner. This could be achieved by, e.g., applying costly cryptographic methods. Therefore, we evaluate the potential effects of this type of information sharing on the whole supply chain by developing a simulation model representing a 4-echelon supply chain, which consists of multiple customers, each served by their own retailer, one wholesaler, one manufacturer and one supplier. We investigate the effect of horizontal information sharing of average customer demand among the retailers on the bullwhip effect and show its effects on the different supply chain actors. In addition, the influences of demand variability and demand correlations between customers, different forecasting methods, safety stock approaches, stochastic lead times and the number of retailers sharing the information on the bullwhip effect are analyzed.

## 3. Balancing and scheduling of transfer lines for cycle time and peak power minimisation: a learning-enhanced multi-objective variable neighbourhood search

Pedram Beldar, Ehsan Mahmoodi, Mostafa Kermani, Gary Linnéusson, Olga Battaïa. International Journal of Production Research, 2026-10-05. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: University of Skövde; Kedge Business School (FR, SE)
Lanes: Production planning and scheduling, AI in planning. Score 8.2: Nordic/DACH authors (SE); established journal; practice signals: industrial; reports a quantified result.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2026.2741382 · Full text: papers/W7220372069.txt

As global electricity demand surges due to industrial electrification and digitalisation, peak power (PP) management has emerged as a critical challenge in energy-intensive manufacturing, affecting operational costs and grid capacity. PP is defined as the maximum instantaneous total power demand across all workstations during the production cycle, and its minimisation is essential for reducing demand charges and avoiding grid overloads. This study introduces the balancing and scheduling of machining transfer lines as a bi-objective optimisation problem that simultaneously minimises cycle time (CT) and PP under precedence, inclusion/exclusion, and technological compatibility constraints. A multi-objective constraint programming (CP) model is formulated and solved via a bidirectional ε-constraint method, generating high-quality Pareto sets for small-scale instances. To overcome the computational intractability of CP for medium- and large-scale problems, a multi-objective variable neighbourhood search (MOVNS) enhanced with deep reinforcement learning (NN_MOVNS) is proposed, in which a Deep Q-Network adaptively guides neighbourhood operator selection throughout the search. Computational experiments on 55 benchmark instances demonstrate that NN_MOVNS consistently outperforms competing algorithms across all problem scales, with improvements becoming increasingly pronounced on larger instances, as confirmed by statistical significance tests. Peak shaving experiments confirm PP reductions ranging from 33% to 73% with modest CT increases averaging 5.7%. An industrial-scale test instance of a 148-task automotive cylinder head production line achieves an 11.8% CT reduction and a 63.1% PP reduction, lowering the peak-to-average power ratio from 2.35 to 1.49.

## 4. Mitigating supply chain transparency barriers with blockchain: a dynamic capabilities perspective

Eric Ragan, Mohammad H. Eslami. International Journal of Production Research, 2026-10-02. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: University of Gothenburg; University of Manitoba (CA, SE)
Lanes: Resilience and risk. Score 8.0: Nordic/DACH authors (SE); established journal; practice signals: case study, empirical, swedish.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2026.2738915 · Full text: papers/W7216198559.txt

The complexity and risk uncertainty stemming from supply disruptions underscore the need for enhanced transparency in supply chain management. Although blockchain technologies offer promising solutions through secure, immutable ledgers, adoption rates remain low, and extant research focuses primarily on theoretical frameworks lacking empirical substantiation. Addressing this gap, the present study reconceptualises transparency barriers as capability frictions, systematic impediments that prevent firms from effectively deploying sensing, seizing, and transformation capabilities. Through an in-depth case study of a Swedish OEM and its suppliers, four transparency barriers are identified: information inaccuracy, risks of information sharing, lack of communication protocols, and future communications compatibility. Analysis demonstrates how these barriers create capability frictions by weakening sensing through information asymmetry, constraining seizing through limited collaboration, and restricting transformation through weak data governance. The findings reveal how blockchain's distinctive properties, immutability, transparency, and decentralised consensus, systematically reduce these frictions, enabling firms to transition from weakened to enhanced dynamic capabilities. Five propositions are developed theorising the mechanisms through which blockchain serves as a digital enabler for capability transformation, contributing to dynamic capabilities theory and supply chain management practice.

## 5. Energy- and labour-aware scheduling under uncertainty: a decomposition framework for stochastic batch-processing systems

Balwin Bokor, Roland Braune, Klaus Altendorfer, Ştefan Wagner. International Journal of Production Research, 2026-10-28. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: University of Applied Sciences Upper Austria; University of Vienna (AT)
Lanes: Production planning and scheduling. Score 7.7: Nordic/DACH authors (AT); established journal; practice signals: manufacturer; reports a quantified result.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2026.2752510 · Full text: papers/W7224456260.txt

Motivated by the energy-intensive heat-treatment operations of a European lead-acid battery manufacturer, this study addresses energy- and labour-aware scheduling for a stochastic batch-processing system with waiting-time constraints and programme-compatibility requirements. We formalise the problem as a two-level optimisation decomposed into five sub-decisions and design a generic scheduling framework spanning from simple Rule-Based Heuristics (RH) to rules automatically synthesised by Genetic Programming (GP) for a single sub-decision. All approaches are evaluated in an agent-based discrete-event simulation mirroring the company's production. Optimising all five sub-decisions using handcrafted rules (Best RH) significantly reduces costs by 40.8% at low and 85.4% at high utilisation over Current Practice, with substantially improved solution robustness. Substituting a single sub-decision with GP-evolved rules reveals both what evolved rules achieve alone and what potential handcrafted rules leave. At high utilisation, it yields a significant 41.9% cost reduction over Current Practice, whereas on the already optimised Best RH the cost gain is marginal and non-significant – but robustness still improves markedly, a critical advantage in stochastic environments. The central contribution is methodological: partitioning the problem by decision structure rather than machine structure reduces complexity enough for handcrafted rules to remain competitive with evolved ones.

## 6. Learning from forecast revisions for inventory replenishment under non-stationary demand

Saadinur Azisbek uulu, Yılmaz Uygun. Operations Research Perspectives, 2026-10-02. article, licence cc-by-nc-nd, cited 0.
Institutions: Constructor University (DE)
Lanes: Forecasting, Inventory, AI in planning. Score 7.5: Nordic/DACH authors (DE); established journal; reports a quantified result.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orp.2026.100419 · Full text: papers/W7216078518.txt

Forecast revisions contain information about emerging demand changes that is typically discarded in inventory-control policies. Our two main contributions are (i) a POMDP state augmentation that pairs the history of forecast revisions with Bayesian change-point and forecast-uncertainty signals, and (ii) FR-PPO (Forecast Revision Proximal Policy Optimization), a two-level recurrent architecture that encodes this revision history through a GRU inside a RecurrentPPO controller centered on an uncertainty-adjusted base-stock reference. We evaluated FR-PPO against two classical and six reinforcement learning benchmarks in five synthetic demand scenarios and seven regime-active M5 SKUs. FR-PPO is the best PPO-family policy in all five synthetic scenarios and achieves the lowest overall cost on three of them, reducing cost by 72.2% relative to adaptive BaseStock under seasonal demand while reducing adaptation lag by 63%. The method is not uniformly dominant: off-policy SAC baselines retain an advantage under sparse, intermittent demand, delimiting where the revision signal is most valuable. On real M5 data, FR-PPO reduces cost relative to adaptive BaseStock on all seven SKUs (4.2%–23.0%). These findings identify forecast-revision histories as useful, low-cost decision information for replenishment under demand non-stationarity.

## 7. A systematic review of the collaborative intelligence in multi-agent scheduling

Kuo‐Ching Ying, Pourya Pourhejazy, Bo-Cheng Chen. International Journal of Production Research, 2026-11-08. article, licence cc-by-nc-nd, cited 0.
Institutions: National Taipei University of Technology; UiT The Arctic University of Norway; Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (Taiwan) (NO, TW)
Lanes: Production planning and scheduling. Score 7.4: Nordic/DACH authors (NO); established journal; practice signals: field, real-world.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2026.2752521 · Full text: papers/W7225561281.txt

Traditional scheduling assumes a single actor, while real-world scenarios often involve multiple decision-making units with conflicting objectives, requiring collaboration, coordination, negotiation, and collective intelligence. Multi-agent scheduling (MAS) provides a realistic framework for modelling such interactions under shared resources. This article presents a comprehensive scientometric and systematic review of two-agent and multi-agent scheduling, examining the field’s development paths, knowledge structure, and emerging research clusters. Main path, cluster, and keyword analyses are integrated to trace knowledge diffusion, identify influential studies and research branches, and examine how collaborative intelligence is manifested across scheduling contexts. The findings reveal an evolution from early complexity-oriented single-machine and dual-agent models toward richer production environments, advanced computational methods, and application-oriented MAS in smart manufacturing, energy systems, edge computing, and decentralised project scheduling. More importantly, the synthesis reveals a broader transition from optimizing competing agent-specific objectives toward interaction-driven collaborative scheduling intelligence based on coordination, negotiation, information sharing, adaptation, and collective learning. This domain-level perspective suggests that future MAS should integrate decentralised decision-making, adaptive intelligence, and system-level coordination to reconcile local objectives with system-wide performance.

Disaster relief logistics presents major challenges. Uncertain demand, scarce resources, disrupted transportation networks, and rapidly evolving conditions make equitable and timely distribution extremely difficult. This paper proposes a hybrid robust-fuzzy two-stage programming framework for the procurement, allocation, and inventory management of essential relief commodities. A rolling horizon approach continuously updates decisions using real-time demand and inventory data, enhancing adaptability and responsiveness. The study makes three key contributions. We consider long-lead forecasting of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) climate phenomenon. This is widely seen as a challenging task from a methodological perspective. At the same time, ENSO events are of substantial societal importance. We adopt a class of score-driven models to capture the key dynamic features of the Niño 3.4 index and include a large set of previously designed predictors. ## 12. Managing geopolitical risk in global supply chains: A qualitative study of European multinational enterprises

Arnd Huchzermeier, Mark Kuppek. International Journal of Production Economics, 2026-09-23. article, licence cc-by-nc-nd, cited 0.
Institutions: WHU – Otto Beisheim School of Management (DE)
Lanes: Resilience and risk. Score 6.0: Nordic/DACH authors (DE); established journal.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2026.110226 · No full text saved

Geopolitical risk has emerged as a major disruptive risk to global supply chains and the operations of multinational enterprises (MNEs) today. Drawing on a qualitative content analysis of the annual reports of 20 European MNEs across five industries, supported by a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline, this study examines how companies strategically manage geopolitical risk in their supply chains. We extend an established supply chain risk framework by conceptualizing geopolitical risk management (GRM) as a distinct strategic risk management dimension centered on reducing geopolitical exposure, in contrast to supply chain risk management (SCRM), which enhances operational resilience within existing supply chain structures. Applying this distinction, we identify seven key GRM strategies, including localization, decentralized procurement and R&D, direct sourcing, supplier diversification, geographical production diversification, circular economy initiatives, and political engagement. Furthermore, applying the Triple-P framework in a disclosure-based setting suggests that companies facing different forms of supply chain complexity across the partnership, product, and process dimensions disclose distinct portfolios of GRM strategies. The findings of this study provide cross-industry insights into the strategic orientation of large European MNEs and support managerial decision making to strengthen resilience in the face of supply chain and geopolitical risk.

## 13. Agents of collaborative adaptation: modelling temporal resilience in supply chains

Ari Carisza Graha Prasetia. Annals of Operations Research, 2026-09-03. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: KTH Royal Institute of Technology (SE)
Lanes: Resilience and risk. Score 6.0: Nordic/DACH authors (SE); established journal.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-026-07406-4 · No full text saved

Abstract Supply-chain disruptions vary in intensity, scale, and frequency, producing temporally heterogeneous impacts on network viability. The emphasis on exposure and topology has anchored resilience in network structure, obscuring how collaboration and trust govern its phase-specific temporal dynamics, including resistive capabilities, adaptation speed, containment, and recovery. This study thus develops an agent-based simulation of the Netherlands logistics network, in which nodes operate as autonomous agents that redistribute load under capacity, link, and trust constraints. Cascading failures are simulated with trust-gated, capacity-aware, and collaboration-dependent load reallocation, capturing both immediate contagion and multi-step redistribution dynamics. The framework compares rule-based heuristics with adaptive AI agents trained using supervised imitation, online multiplicative adaptation, and cross-entropy policy search. Temporal resilience is then measured across each phase specific metrics of absorptive, adaptive, and recovery phases across the collaboration structure. This points to the main contribution of this article, in which AI agents improve adaptation and cascade containment through targeted use of high-trust, high-capacity links, while recovery dynamics remain bounded by structural and exogenous constraints. This points to a layered view of resilience, where performance depends on the interaction between network configuration, trust-mediated coordination, and agent-level adaptation.

## 14. Collaborative routing problems in automated warehouses

Julian Golak, Lara Nehrke, Dominik Kress, Malte Fliedner. European Journal of Operational Research, 2026-09-01. article, licence cc-by, cited 0.
Institutions: Universität Hamburg; Helmut Schmidt University (DE)
Lanes: Production planning and scheduling. Score 6.0: Nordic/DACH authors (DE); established journal.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2026.09.002 · No full text saved

The pick-and-drive system is a novel solution for warehouse automation. It integrates two types of autonomous mobile robots (AMRs). Storage and supply AMRs (S-AMRs) navigate to a storage area, where they lift unit loads and transport them to a disposition area. Picking AMRs (P-AMRs) move freely within this disposition area. They carry bins and meet S-AMRs to collect items from the unit loads attached to the S-AMRs by means of robotic arms. We take an operational perspective on the pick-and-drive system and focus on the problem of determining and scheduling meeting locations and sequences of the AMRs, so that all required items are picked. This setting is interpreted as a vehicle routing problem, where the customers (S-AMRs) collaborate with the vehicles (P-AMRs). We consider two objective functions, minimizing the total distance driven by the AMRs and minimizing the sum of completion times of the P-AMRs. We translate these routing problems into a mathematical framework and provide theoretical insights by examining a specific case where the potential meeting points lie along a line. For this scenario, we present an approximation algorithm for the case of minimizing the overall distance traveled and demonstrate that minimizing the sum of completion times is strongly NP-complete. Additionally, we prove strong NP-completeness of the general case when minimizing the overall distance traveled. Leveraging the structural insights, we develop heuristics that are evaluated in a numerical study. Our results highlight the potential impact of the system in practice.

Abstract Accurately predicting fast solar wind conditions is challenging, as uncertainties are large and unquantified by traditional single‐value prediction models. In particular, the risks of high‐speed solar wind streams (HSSs), which can cause damage to technological infrastructure, cannot be reliably assessed without probabilistic forecasts. We present PROSWIN, a probabilistic machine learning model that forecasts the hourly solar wind speed (SWS) at Earth with a 4‐day lead time. The approach combines solar images and magnetograms using a deep neural network coupled to a distributional regression algorithm. Because standard error metrics underweight the relevance of HSS peaks, we further introduce the prediction score (PS) , a model‐selection metric that jointly rewards timeline and HSS peak accuracy. These results support the advantages of probabilistic over single‐value solar wind models. The introduced methods are also transferable to other forecasting problems.

## 17. Optimizing freight rail electrification: A framework for charge station selection and battery charge/swap scheduling

Institutions: UNSW Sydney (AU)
Lanes: Inventory, Production planning and scheduling. Score 5.3: established journal; practice signals: case study; reports a quantified result.
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tre.2026.105305 · No full text saved

Battery-electric freight trains are promising for decarbonization by providing zero-emission transportation alternatives. Successful adoption of battery-electric freight trains depends on an efficient electrification strategy, involving infrastructure setup and charging scheduling. The study presents a comprehensive model for the optimal design of charging infrastructure and charge scheduling for trains. To provide greater recharging flexibility, we allow a battery to be either charged or swapped at a deployed station, and each train can carry multiple batteries. This problem is formulated as a mixed-integer linear programming model with a Rectangle Piecewise Linear Approximation (PLA) technique.
