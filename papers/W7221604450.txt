Abstract
This study addresses the multi-crane scheduling problem with temporal constraints 
(MCSPTC) arising in steelmaking shops, where multiple rail-guided overhead 
cranes transport materials on shared tracks and must strictly avoid interference. 
The problem requires jointly determining task assignment and sequencing deci -
sions while satisfying temporal constraints on task start times, including time win -
dows and precedence relations. In practice, manufacturing execution system data 
are updated frequently, requiring high quality solutions to be generated repeatedly 
within short computation times. However, existing heuristics lack quality guaran -
tees, while exact models do not scale to industrial-size instances within practical 
time limits. To tackle these challenges, we formulate the MCSPTC as a sequential 
decision process and propose a Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS)–based solution 
framework. The proposed approach performs look-ahead planning at each decision 
point using a schedule construction mechanism. To improve search efficiency, we 
develop a feasibility-based action pruning strategy that eliminates actions inevitably 
leading to infeasible schedules prior to node expansion. We further tailor the value 
backup and final action selection rules to account for the tight temporal feasibility 
and the varying size of the feasible action space induced by temporal constraints. 
Our numerical results suggest that the proposed MCTS framework yields better 
solutions than existing heuristics within short computation times. In particular, the 
proposed method improves upon the heuristic baselines by approximately 5%–7% 
for both small and medium-scale instances within one minute and large-scale in -
stances within about three minutes.

1 Introduction
Efficient scheduling of material handling equipment plays a critical role in the opera-
tional performance of modern manufacturing systems (Boysen et al. 2017; Fragapane 
et al. 2021). In steelmaking shops, rail-guided overhead cranes are essential resources 
responsible for transporting heavy materials, such as ladles, between production units 
(Shin et al. 2025). Since these cranes directly connect major processing stages, their 
scheduling decisions have a significant impact on production flow, equipment utiliza-
tion, and overall system stability.
This study addresses the multi-crane scheduling problem arising in steelmaking 
shops, where multiple cranes operate on shared tracks and perform a sequence of 
material transportation tasks. Each task corresponds to moving a material from one 
location to another and must be executed by a designated crane within a limited time 
window determined by upstream and downstream production processes. As cranes 
travel along common rails, interference between cranes must be strictly avoided, fur-
ther complicating the scheduling process (Li et al. 2020). Consequently, the resulting 
problem couples task assignment and sequencing decisions with interference avoid -
ance and temporal feasibility. We refer to this problem as the multi-crane scheduling 
problem with temporal constraints (MCSPTC).
The MCSPTC is characterized by temporal constraints, including release times, 
deadlines, and precedence relations derived from pre-planned production sched -
ules. The objective is to determine feasible start times for transportation tasks that 
minimize a given performance measure, such as total flow time, while satisfying all 
temporal and interference constraints. Although a formal mathematical definition is 
provided later in the paper, the problem naturally exhibits a sequential decision struc-
ture, in which each dispatching decision alters future feasibility and performance.
Practical operational environments impose additional challenges beyond the 
intrinsic complexity of the problem. Based on observations from the steelmaking 
plant investigated in this study, manufacturing execution system (MES) data are 
updated at a minute-level resolution, reflecting frequent changes in production plans, 
machine states, and task availability. Consequently, the long-horizon schedule can -
not be maintained in a fixed manner and must be continuously updated to reflect 
up-to-date MES snapshots. This frequent re-scheduling requires an approach that can 
deliver solutions within short computational time. Moreover, from an operational 
standpoint, algorithms whose runtimes vary significantly across instances often fail 
to gain trust from field engineers, even when high-quality solutions are occasionally 
obtained.
Several studies have attempted to address the MCSPTC using optimization-based 
and heuristic approaches. For example, Shin and Kim ( 2025) proposed two mixed-
integer linear programming (MILP) formulations together with solution-construction 
heuristics to generate feasible crane schedules. While these heuristics are capable of 
producing solutions extremely quickly, their solution quality is often unsatisfactory 
due to their inherent simplicity. Subsequent research (Shin and Kim 2026) introduced 
enhanced MILP formulations that significantly improve modeling accuracy and solu-
tion quality. However, despite these improvements, such approaches remain funda -
mentally limited by the computational burden of large-scale mathematical models,

preventing their use in time-critical operational settings. Similar limitations have 
been recognized more broadly in the multi-crane scheduling literature across differ -
ent industrial domains, where the trade-off between solution quality and computa -
tional efficiency has motivated ongoing research efforts into more effective solution 
approaches (Zhao et al. 2019; Jin et al. 2024). Nevertheless, studies that explicitly 
address temporal constraints in multi-crane scheduling remain limited.
Motivated by this gap, the objective of this study is to develop a crane scheduling 
method that delivers high-quality solutions for industrial-scale instances under short 
and consistent computational time limits. To this end, this study formulates MCSPTC 
as a sequential decision process and proposes a Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS)-
based solution framework that explores alternative dispatching decisions in a look-
ahead manner. MCTS is a simulation-based planning method that has demonstrated 
strong performance in sequential decision problems. In the MCSPTC, task time 
windows and precedence relations naturally induce a desirable decision order, mak -
ing many decision sequences clearly inefficient. MCTS can leverage this structure 
by allocating more search effort to promising sequences while avoiding excessive 
computation on less favorable ones, evaluating each candidate through rollouts that 
simulate schedule completion and thus yield reliable quality estimates even where 
a partial schedule’s quality cannot be directly calculated. MCTS further allows the 
search to return the best solution found within an allocated resource limit, naturally 
accommodating the time-critical nature of the scheduling task.
A key feature of the proposed method is that the tree search is guided by a sched-
ule construction mechanism previously developed for this problem class (Shin and 
Kim 2025). This mechanism is used both to simulate state transitions and to evalu -
ate rollout trajectories, enabling effective exploration of the solution space through 
MCTS. Beyond the standard MCTS framework, we introduce two additional design 
components that reflect the tight temporal feasibility and precedence structure of the 
MCSPTC. First, to further enhance search efficiency, we develop a feasibility-based 
action pruning strategy that removes actions inevitably leading to infeasible sched -
ules prior to node expansion. Second, to prevent frequent infeasible rollouts from 
distorting value estimates, we adopt a best-return backup strategy that updates node 
values based on the best observed return, and design a final action selection rule that 
adapts to the size of the feasible action space induced by precedence relations.
Comprehensive computational experiments demonstrate that the proposed MCTS 
framework substantially improves solution quality compared with existing heuristic 
approaches. In particular, for small- and medium-scale instances, MCTS achieves 
a median improvement of approximately 5 to 7% over the heuristic baselines with 
solution times under one minute, while for large-scale instances the improvement 
remains approximately 6 to 7%, with solution times of approximately three minutes 
or less, thereby elevating the method’s suitability for deployment in real-world steel-
making operations.

2 Related works
This section reviews the relevant literature in four parts. First, we introduce sev -
eral multi-crane scheduling problems (MCSPs) and their major application domains. 
Second, we review recent studies applying MCTS to crane scheduling and related 
combinatorial optimization problems. Third, we summarize existing MCSP studies 
incorporating temporal constraints. Finally, we discuss prior work that addresses the 
same MCSPTC considered in this study and clarify the research gap.
2.1 Multi-crane scheduling problems
MCSPs have been widely studied in industrial systems where rail-guided cranes 
serve as primary material handling equipment. These cranes transport heavy loads 
along predefined tracks and are commonly deployed in container terminals, logistics 
warehouses, manufacturing warehouses, and production facilities.
In container terminals, quay cranes transfer containers between vessels and the 
dock (Kim and Park 2004; Diabat and Theodorou 2014; Wang et al. 2025a), whereas 
yard cranes support stacking and retrieval operations in storage yards to ensure 
smooth cargo flow (Ng 2005; Huang and Li 2017; Wang et al. 2025b; Liu et al. 2026). 
Comprehensive surveys on crane operations and scheduling in container terminals, 
covering both quay and yard crane settings, are provided by Kizilay and Eliiyi (2021).
In logistics warehouses, rail-guided cranes are core components of automated 
storage and retrieval systems, enabling high-throughput order fulfillment (Kung 
et al. 2014; Heshmati et al. 2019). In manufacturing warehouses, cranes handle raw 
materials and semi-finished products, often under energy-related considerations 
and dynamically changing operating conditions (Peng et al. 2021; Cho et al. 2024; 
Weckenborg et al. 2025; Dong et al. 2025). In factory environments, cranes further 
facilitate material transportation between machines, thereby supporting continuous 
production (Peterson et al. 2014; Li et al. 2020).
As reviewed above, MCSPs and the MCSPTC share a foundational structural core. 
Both problems require jointly determining task assignment and sequencing decisions

methodology proposed in this study.
2.2 MCTS-based approaches for crane scheduling
MCTS was proposed by Coulom (2006), who combined tree search with Monte Carlo 
evaluation, and was later enhanced by Kocsis and Szepesvári ( 2006) through the 
introduction of the bandit-based upper confidence bounds for trees (UCT) selection 
strategy. MCTS has since been widely adopted across diverse complex domains (Sil-
ver et al. 2016; Segler et al. 2018), and has also been adapted to combinatorial opti -
mization and scheduling problems where exhaustive search is intractable (Browne 
et al. 2012; Salami et al. 2026). This widespread adoption is owing to its ability to 
return anytime solutions while balancing exploration and exploitation under a con -
trollable computational budget. Most relevant to this work is its recent application 
to crane scheduling. Specifically, Jooken et al. (2023) integrated search-acceleration 
mechanisms, such as subtree pruning, heuristic simulation, and domain reduction, 
into MCTS, achieving competitive performance on the quay crane scheduling prob -
lem with non-crossing constraints.
More recently, Hai et al. (2026) developed a self-adaptive MCTS algorithm for a 
generalized quay crane scheduling problem, introducing problem-specific techniques 
including adaptive lower bounds for node selection and subtree pruning. Their results 
showed that MCTS can outperform existing heuristics while achieving solution qual-
ity comparable to advanced optimization-based methods. These studies indicate the 
potential of MCTS as an effective search paradigm for large-scale and time-critical 
crane scheduling environments. While these studies confirm the effectiveness of 
MCTS for crane scheduling, they target quay crane settings and do not address the 
strict time windows that characterize the MCSPTC, leaving the design of MCTS for 
this more constrained setting as an open question.
2.3 Temporal constraints in crane scheduling
Several studies have investigated MCSPs under various forms of temporal con -
straints, although their modeling assumptions differ from those considered in this 
study. In quay crane scheduling problems, tasks typically correspond to container 
groups, and precedence relations are commonly derived from vessel stowage plans 
(Kim and Park 2004). Crane availability may also be restricted by time windows 
reflecting operational shifts or maintenance schedules (Meisel 2011; Legato et al. 
2012).
In yard crane scheduling problems, tasks are often defined as individual container 
handover operations. In such settings, truck arrival times induce task release times 
(Ng 2005; Li et al. 2009; Guo et al. 2018), while deadlines are imposed based on 
truck waiting limits or vessel departure schedules (Eilken 2019; Zhou et al. 2026). 
Temporal dependencies may further arise from stacking configurations or crane-to-

crane handover requirements, giving rise to precedence relations among tasks (Kress 
et al. 2019).
Temporal constraints have also been incorporated into MCSPs arising in manufac-
turing and storage systems. In slab yards and coil warehouses, precedence relations 
are primarily determined by stacking positions and material accessibility (Dohn and 
Clausen 2010; Cho et al. 2024). In factory environments, precedence constraints typi-
cally follow the production routes of materials through multiple machines (Hirsch 
et al. 2012; Peterson et al. 2014; Li et al. 2020). In these studies, deadlines are often 
derived from machine cycle times (Hirsch et al. 2012), while release times and due 
dates are specified according to factory-level operational rules (Li et al. 2020).
2.4 Prior work on the MCSPTC
To the best of our knowledge, only a limited number of studies have addressed the 
multi-crane scheduling problem with temporal constraints (MCSPTC) under the 
same problem setting considered in this study. Shin and Kim ( 2025) introduced the 
MCSPTC by incorporating both generalized precedence relations and strict time win-
dows derived from production plans in a steelmaking plant. That study developed 
multiple MILP models and schedule generation schemes to evaluate modeling effec-
tiveness and computational performance using real production data.
Subsequently, Shin and Kim ( 2026) extended the MCSPTC framework by sig -
nificantly enhancing the modeling of crane interference. In particular, an existing 
closed-form non-interference model, previously limited to stationary-task settings, 
was generalized to accommodate transportation tasks involving crane movements 
across multiple locations. The study further incorporated temporal constraints and 
proposed an MILP formulation strengthened by valid inequalities derived from net -
work analysis.
Although these studies established the fundamental modeling framework of the 
MCSPTC and demonstrated its effectiveness using exact optimization approaches, 
their solution methodologies primarily rely on MILP-based formulations or relatively 
simple heuristics. As a result, scalability limitations and unpredictable computation 
times may arise in large-scale or time-sensitive operational environments, motivating 
the development of alternative solution paradigms.
Building on these observations, this study develops an MCTS-based framework 
that, unlike exact MILP models or simple construction heuristics, attains high solu -
tion quality within short and consistent computational time. Beyond a direct appli -
cation of MCTS, the framework incorporates feasibility-based action pruning and 
problem-specific search rules tailored to the temporal-feasibility and precedence 
structure of the MCSPTC. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first look-ahead 
search framework for the MCSPTC that systematically explores a complex and broad 
search space.

4 Solution approach
This section presents the proposed MCTS-based solution framework for the MCSPTC. 
The problem involves tightly coupled decisions on task sequencing and task–crane 
assignment under crane interference constraints. Moreover, the temporal constraints 
strongly shape both the structure and performance of the decision sequence, as early 
dispatching choices can substantially restrict the flexibility of subsequent deci -
sions and degrade solution quality. Consequently, unguided exploration of decision 
sequences can be computationally inefficient, as many candidates quickly become 
infeasible or uncompetitive. Leveraging simulation-based look-ahead, MCTS can 
concentrate its search on promising branches while limiting effort on less promising 
ones under a prescribed computational budget.
To this end, we formulate the MCSPTC as a sequential decision process and 
develop an MCTS framework tailored to this setting. The proposed method integrates 
schedule generation schemes, feasibility-based action pruning, and customized value 
backup and final action selection rules to achieve high solution quality with reliable 
runtime behavior.
This section is organized as follows. We first formulate the MCSPTC as a sequen-
tial decision process by defining the state, action, and transition models. We then 
introduce two schedule-generation-based transition mechanisms. Next, we present

4.2 Feasibility-based action pruning
The naive action space may contain a large number of task–crane pairs that inevitably 
lead to infeasible schedules. Exploring such actions not only wastes computational 
effort, but also degrades the effectiveness of the tree search. To improve search effi -
ciency, we restrict the action space using feasibility-based pruning rules.
First, for each candidate action (𝜏𝜏𝜏 𝜏𝜏𝜏, a one-step feasibility check is performed. 
Specifically, the Step-I transition is tentatively applied to verify whether a feasible 
successor state can be constructed. Only actions for which Step-I yields a feasible 
state are retained in the action space. This screening also prevents obviously infea -
sible children from entering the tree, thereby avoiding the propagation of infeasi -
bility-induced penalties to the statistics of their ancestor nodes and stabilizing the 
search. Importantly, this proactive filtering incurs minimal computational overhead, 
as it requires only a single Step-I evaluation per candidate action.

After B iterations, the final action at the root node must be selected. The action 
associated with the largest Q value is chosen. Unlike visit-count-based selection, 
which selects the most frequently visited action, this criterion avoids bias toward 
nodes with larger branching factors. Such bias commonly arises in the MCSPTC 
because tasks do not share a uniform number of immediate successors, resulting in 
heterogeneous subtree sizes. Consequently, nodes with larger subtrees tend to accu -
mulate higher visit counts even when they are not more promising. Selecting the 
action with the best Q value provides a more reliable decision rule in this setting. This 
is likewise supported by the ablation study, in which visit-count-based selection (the 
MV variant) yields consistently worse solutions (Sect. 5.6, Figs. 9, 10).

tion to update the current schedule, and the entire MCTS procedure is repeated from 
the resulting state until a terminal state is reached.
5 Computational experiments
This section presents computational experiments conducted to evaluate the effec -
tiveness of the proposed MCTS-based solution framework for the MCSPTC. The 
experiments focus on assessing solution quality, computational efficiency, and scal -
ability under realistic operational conditions derived from industrial production data. 
We first describe the benchmark instances and experimental settings, and then report 
comparative results against existing heuristic and optimization-based approaches. 
Finally, additional experiments are conducted to analyze the impact of key compo -
nents of the proposed MCTS framework.
5.1 Problem instances
The problem instances used in this study are based on the benchmark set introduced 
in Shin and Kim (2026), which was constructed using production data collected from 
an actual steelmaking plant. These instances reflect the operational characteristics 
observed in practice, including crane layouts, transportation distances, task arrival 
patterns, and temporal constraints derived from the production plan.
More specifically, each job corresponds to a batch of molten steel processed 
through several production stages, yielding multiple transportation tasks. For a given 
number of jobs, the machine assignments and processing times are sampled from real 
production parameters, which are then used to construct the tasks, their attributes, 
and the associated temporal constraints. For the detailed generation procedure, we 
refer the reader to Shin and Kim (2026).
The plant operates multiple rail-guided cranes on two parallel tracks. Transporta -
tion tasks correspond to material movements between processing machines located 
1 3
W.-J. Shin et al.
along each track, and crane interference may occur when cranes operate within over-
lapping spatial regions. To capture different operational scales, the instance set is 
structured along two dimensions: layout scale and time scale.
Regarding layout scale, two configurations are considered. In the 2-crane layout, 
each track is served by two cranes, whereas in the 3-crane layout, three cranes operate 
on each track. These two settings correspond to actual crane configurations operated 
in the plant, and this performance evaluation aims to verify the proposed method 
under different settings. As introduced by Shin and Kim (2026), both the crane counts 
and spatial constraints were configured to faithfully mirror the plant’s actual operat -
ing environment.
With respect to time scale, instances are categorized into three groups according 
to the number of jobs. Each job consists of 4–5 transportation tasks. Small-scale 
instances consist of 5–25 jobs, corresponding to approximately 1–6 h of production. 
Medium-scale instances include 30–50 jobs, representing 6–12 h of operation, while 
large-scale instances contain 60–100 jobs and approximate a full production day of 
12–24 h.
Following the benchmark design of Shin and Kim ( 2026), we evaluate the pro -
posed method only on the available combinations of layout and time scales. The orig-
inal benchmark provides small-, medium-, and large-scale instances for the 2-crane 
layout, but only small- and medium-scale instances for the 3-crane layout. In that 
benchmark, the primary target setting is the 2-crane layout with small- and medium-
scale instances; the large-scale 2-crane instances extend the time scale to test robust-
ness over longer planning horizons, whereas the 3-crane instances extend the layout 
scale to assess performance under an increased number of cranes. Therefore, we fol-
low the same benchmark scope in this study.
All instances share the same basic parameter settings. The unit travel time is set 
to ̂𝑡𝑡𝑡𝑡 , and the loading and unloading time is fixed to 𝜆𝜆𝜆𝜆 . A minimum safety dis-
tance between adjacent cranes is enforced by setting the crane separation parameter 
to 𝛾𝛾𝛾𝛾 . In the 2-crane layout, cranes initially start at positions 6 and 12 on each 
track, with task source and target locations distributed between positions 0 and 18. In 
the 3-crane layout, cranes start at positions 6, 12, and 18, and task source and target 
locations are generated within the range from 0 to 24.
For each combination of layout scale and time scale, 30 independent random 
instances are generated and shared across all solution methods, so that every method 
is evaluated on the same instances, resulting in a comprehensive benchmark set that 
enables statistically meaningful performance evaluation across a wide range of oper-
ating conditions.

5.8 Managerial insights
The experimental results suggest that the most appropriate solution methodology 
strongly depends on the operational context, particularly the planning horizon and 
the frequency of data updates. In a minute-level MES environment where production 
data change frequently, schedules must be regenerated within short, repeated cycles. 
In such settings, achieving a consistent and controllable computation time becomes 
as critical as ensuring solution quality.
The proposed MCTS framework offers a highly practical balance for these real-
time operations. It consistently improves solution quality over the heuristic baselines 
while maintaining short and stable runtimes. Conversely, the MILP approach is less 
suitable for continuous online rescheduling due to its long and highly variable solu -
tion times.
However, when scheduling is performed over a longer planning horizon or updated 
infrequently, the available computation time becomes less restrictive. In those cases, 
exact methods like MP-ETD+ may be preferred to obtain or certify optimal solutions. 
On the other end of the spectrum, if an immediate response is required or a rough 
schedule suffices, construction heuristics provide near-instant solutions. Neverthe -
less, their feasibility is not guaranteed under tight temporal constraints, and their 
objective values are typically worse than those of MCTS. Crucially, the tunable itera-
tion budget of the proposed MCTS allows practitioners to flexibly adjust the balance 
between solution quality and computation time based on the available decision cycle, 
making it a highly adaptable option across diverse operational conditions.
6 Conclusion
This study addressed the MCSPTC arising in steelmaking shops, where multiple 
rail-guided cranes operate on shared tracks and must satisfy strict non-interference 
and temporal feasibility requirements. The problem couples task assignment and 
sequencing decisions with crane movement and temporal constraints, and practical 
environments further require solutions to be generated repeatedly within reliable and 
predictable computation times.
To tackle these challenges, we formulated the MCSPTC as a sequential decision-
making problem and proposed an MCTS-based solution framework that performs 
look-ahead planning at each dispatching decision using a schedule construction 
mechanism embedded in both state transitions and rollouts. Rather than applying 
MCTS off the shelf, we adapted its value backup and final action selection rules to the 
tight temporal feasibility and the precedence-induced variation in the feasible action 
space. We further introduced a feasibility-based action pruning strategy that couples 
a one-step feasibility check with a dominance rule provably discarding only actions 
that cannot belong to an optimal schedule, shrinking the search space without sac -
rificing optimality. Crucially, by fixing the per-decision iteration budget in advance, 
the framework delivers solution quality competitive with exact methods while ensur-
ing the consistent and controllable runtimes required for repeated executions.

Computational experiments on benchmark instances derived from real produc -
tion data demonstrate that the proposed method substantially improves solution 
quality over an existing heuristic baseline while maintaining stable runtimes. Near-
optimal solutions are obtained within a few seconds for small- and medium-scale 
instances, and high-quality solutions are produced within a few minutes for large-
scale instances, highlighting the practicality of the proposed framework for time-
critical industrial scheduling.
Despite these promising results, this study has several limitations. First, the frame-
work relies on the existing SGS-I construction scheme for state transitions and roll -
outs, which inherently constrains the overall solution quality by the performance of 
the underlying heuristic. Second, although the experiments demonstrate the stable 
and controllable runtimes required for online rescheduling, the evaluation was lim -
ited to deterministic benchmark instances. Its behavioral performance in stochastic 
environments, in which production parameters deviate from their nominal values, 
remains an open question.
Future work will focus on mitigating this dependence on a single construction 
scheme by introducing enhanced rollout and evaluation mechanisms. Additionally, 
we plan to evaluate the framework in stochastic operational settings and further refine 
the tree-search efficiency through tighter problem-specific lower bounds and domi -
nance rules.

Constraints ( 2)–(3) assign each task to a feasible crane and account for the initial 
travel time from the crane’s starting position to its first task. Constraints (4)–(5) deter-
mine the relative order of two tasks handled by the same crane and impose the travel-
time-dependent separation between their start times.
Constraints (6)–(7) enforce non-interference via the ETD framework. The set Θ 
contains only task-crane assignment pairs for which mutual interference may occur. 
For each (𝜏𝜏𝜏 𝜏𝜏′𝜏 𝑣𝑣𝜏 𝑣𝑣′) ∈ Θ, the parameter Δ𝑣𝑣𝑣𝑣𝑣′ 𝜏𝜏𝑣𝜏𝜏′ represents the minimum temporal sepa-
ration required between completing task 𝜏𝜏 by crane v and starting task 𝜏𝜏′ by crane 𝑣𝑣′ 
to guarantee non-interference. Appendix A provides the detailed closed-form compu-
tation of Δ𝑣𝑣𝑣𝑣𝑣′ 𝜏𝜏𝑣𝜏𝜏′ following Shin and Kim (2026).
Constraints ( 8)–(10) impose temporal constraints, including time windows and 
precedence relations. Constraint (11) defines the flow time of each objective-related 
task. Since ℎ𝜏𝜏 and 𝑟𝑟𝜏𝜏 are constant parameters, minimizing the total flow time is math-
ematically equivalent to minimizing the total start time of objective-related tasks. 
Finally, constraints (12)–(16) specify the domains of decision variables.
